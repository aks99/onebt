# RESEARCH BRIEF TEMPLATE and the eight standard threads

## The brief (copy, fill every field)

```
Research {specific narrow topic} as it stands in {month year}.

Objective: {what the notes must let a product team decide; name the decisions}

Key questions:
- {question 1, specific, answerable}
- {question 2}
- ... (6–10 questions; each becomes a section of the note)

Suggested sources:
- {primary pages: the platform's own rules/terms/pricing pages, vendor pricing pages, statutes}
- {dated secondary: 2024–2026 industry reports, benchmark datasets, law-firm alerts}
- {first-person accounts: blogs, podcasts, LinkedIn posts, forums}

Constraints:
- Reflect the state as of {date}; label older facts with their dates.
- {geography}; {currency}; quote policy language verbatim where wording matters and cite the URL.
- Record vendor claims as claims. Record fetch summaries as summaries, not quotes.
- If the search budget is exhausted, list the unanswered questions under Gaps; never estimate.

Save your notes to {absolute path}/research_notes/{title}/{thread}.md
Use this format for every key question: ### Takeaway / ### Cited Findings (inline URLs and dates) / ### Inferences / ### Gaps.
```

## The eight standard threads for a SaaS or EdTech product

### 1. Platform ecosystem and rules (run first)
Scale and seasonality (events, listings, users, by region and quarter); how participants are selected or admitted (real forms, fields, timelines, acceptance data, stated rejection reasons); official rules (length, content, payment, rights, branding, publication); terms of use (scraping, datasets, AI); trademark and brand-use policy for third parties with enforcement examples; the official directory or API and what it exposes; organizational changes in the last 24 months.

### 2. Voice of customer and demand
Motivations and reported business outcomes (first-person vs vendor claims); the journey with durations and failure points; effort reported (hours, rehearsals, drafts); aftermath and disappointment; willingness to pay (what people already pay, program price ladders); segment differences; emotional vocabulary verbatim (for copy and onboarding); 20+ indexed first-person accounts with the lesson of each.

### 3. Competitors A: services, coaching, done-for-you
Every vendor with offer, exact price and format, claims, guarantees, criticisms (reviews, BBB, press); the price bands; the labor market that does the job by hand (freelance postings); the ethics line and any scandal.

### 4. Competitors B: software tools
Feature matrix and price table (free-tier limits exactly), metrics measured, technology disclosed, user complaints, funding and pivots, shutdowns; where they fail the target use case.

### 5. Pricing, freemium and go-to-market benchmarks
Conversion benchmarks by model (freemium, trial, reverse trial, card-required) with year and sample; churn by price point; comparable products' tier ladders; how premium tiers justify price (human components); AI cost pass-through patterns (credits, caps, margins); lifecycle packaging (programs, alumni tiers); payments and tax by region (merchant of record, local methods, caps); affiliate norms; funnel benchmarks.

### 6. Design system and UX
Typeface candidates with licenses and variable support; color systems and token architectures; candidate palettes with hex values and computed WCAG ratios in light and dark; layout and navigation patterns for the product shape with measured evidence; AI UX patterns (labelling, diffs, regenerate, confidence, streaming); media UX (recording, review, captions); motivation mechanics with measured effects; accessibility and internationalization; brand-distance rules from the platform's look.

### 7. Technical architecture, costs and compliance
Candidate services with current prices and documented capabilities (STT, LLM, video, vision, storage, jobs, payments, email, analytics); the incumbent stack and whether to keep it; data-pipeline legality (terms, case law); privacy and biometric statutes by region with dates; per-action and per-user cost estimates; infrastructure cost at three scales; effort estimates per module.

### 8. Domain craft and best practices (the content engine)
Named frameworks with steps and attribution; verbatim criteria from the platform and practitioners; benchmark numbers (lengths, rates, counts); checklists; failure modes; constructed rubrics with levels and the source of each criterion.

## What a good thread returns

6,000–12,000 words; 100–200 inline citations; every key question has all four parts; the Gaps section is honest; prices carry the date read; vendor pages are cited directly, not via aggregators where avoidable.
