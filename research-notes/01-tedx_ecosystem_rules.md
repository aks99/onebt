# TEDx Ecosystem, Speaker-Selection Process, Official Rules and Trademark Constraints (state as of late October 2026)

Research note on method: all findings below come from pages fetched 30 Oct 2026 unless the source itself carries an earlier date (noted inline). The TED organizer-guide pages (ted.com/tedx/...) carry no publication/update dates, so their content should be treated as "current as fetched Oct 2026" but of unknown vintage. The web-search budget was exhausted before every sub-question could be searched; those holes are listed under "Gaps" in each section. Where a fetch returned a summary rather than verbatim text, that is stated so the report writer does not present paraphrase as quotation.

## Key Question 1: Scale of TEDx (events/year, by region, total talks, countries, YouTube uploads)

### Takeaway
TED's own most recent published "State of the X" figures (Nov 2023, as reproduced by a licensee) are 3,300+ events in 2023 (~9/day), 42,000+ events since 2009, 180+ countries/territories, 200,000+ talks and ~7B cumulative views; TED's program page now says "More than 4000 events are now held annually," while TED's Feb-2025 transition materials said "more than 3,000" events and "over 250,000 TEDx Talks." As of 26 Oct 2026 the TEDx Talks YouTube channel has ~44.7M subscribers, 272,588 videos and 9.16B views; the official directory listed 2,164 upcoming events on 30 Oct 2026, and an independent Dec-2025 pull of the directory found the US (22%) and India (14%) dominate listings.

### Cited Findings
- TED's TEDx program page (undated): "More than 4000 events are now held annually." Events are "organized independently under a free license granted by TED" and "These events are not controlled by TED." — [TED: TEDx Program](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/tedx-program)
- TED's "State of the X Update November 2023" figures, as reproduced on a licensee page: over 42,000 events held since the program began, in over 180 countries and territories; over 3,300 events in 2023 (about 9 per day); over 600,000 attendees in 2023 to date; TEDx channel talks grew from 400 in 2009 to over 200,000, in over 120 languages; ~7 billion cumulative views, nearly 1 billion of them in 2023; the channel was expected to pass 40 million subscribers in Dec 2023. — [TEDxLinz "What is TEDx" (reproducing TED's Nov 2023 update)](https://tedxlinz.at/whatistedx)
- Feb 4, 2025 figures circulated with Chris Anderson's transition announcement: "More than 3,000 independently run TEDx events," "Over 250,000 TEDx Talks," "a library of more than 4,000 TED Talks," and ~1,700 delegates at the annual Vancouver conference. — [Skift Meetings, Feb 4 2025](https://meetings.skift.com/2025/02/04/chris-anderson-stepping-down-as-the-head-of-ted/)
- Chris Anderson on NPR (late 2025): "there are over 3,000 TEDx events worldwide"; TEDx licenses "were given away to spread the brand." — [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5575174)
- Semafor (Feb 7, 2025): TED's archive "holds more than 250,000 talks, many with tens of millions of views." — [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/02/06/2025/ted-talks-founder-seeks-new-owner-for-famed-conference-platform)
- TEDx Talks YouTube channel stats as of Oct 25–26, 2026: 44,700,000 subscribers; 272,588 total videos; 9,160,835,886 total views; channel created June 23, 2009; derived mean ≈33,600 views per video. — [vidIQ channel stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@tedx/) (Social Blade pages for the channel returned client errors on fetch.)
- TED's official directory on 30 Oct 2026 reported "2,164 upcoming events" ("Showing 1 to 50 of 2,164," 44 pages). — [TED TEDx events directory](https://www.ted.com/tedx/events)
- Independent pull of "every single TEDx event currently listed for 2026" from the TED events map (post dated Dec 3, 2025; modified Feb 11, 2026): 1,365 events listed for 2026. By quarter: Q1 852 (62.4%: Jan 259, Feb 297, Mar 296); Q2 419 (30.7%: Apr 198, May 137, Jun 84); Q3 70 (5.1%); Q4 24 (1.7%). By country: United States 306 (22%), India 190 (14%), Italy 69 (5%), United Kingdom 60 (4%), China 44 (3%), Canada 43 (3%), Türkiye 42 (3%), UAE 37 (2.7%), Brazil 37 (2.7%), Nigeria 26 (1.9%); "The US and India together account for over 36% of events." The author notes the count grows as new licenses are issued (which explains the Q3/Q4 under-count relative to Q1). — [Speaker Accelerator Substack](https://speakeraccelerator.substack.com/p/i-pulled-every-published-tedx-event)
- Historical baseline (TED Blog, Feb 4, 2012): in Jan 2012 there were 77 events in 29 countries; all-time 3,190 events in 800 cities and 126 countries; 12,900 TEDxTalks in the library; 785 new talks uploaded in Jan 2012; 122 TEDx talks featured on TED.com all-time (22 in Jan 2012 alone). — [TED Blog "State of the X"](https://blog.ted.com/state-of-the-x-stats-on-tedx-and-tedxtalks-in-january/)
- TEDxBoulder (organizer claim, undated page): "roughly 40,000 TEDx videos submitted to TED each year" with "less than a 0.1% selection rate" for TED.com. — [TEDxBoulder About](https://tedxboulder.com/about)
- TEDx Talks YouTube channel had "34 million subscribers" in Aug 2022 (newspaper report). — [Surrey Now-Leader, Aug 16 2022](https://www.surreynowleader.com/entertainment/5-surrey-talks-in-teds-global-video-spotlight-as-tedxsurrey-looks-for-more-valuable-and-timely-ideas/)
- Sherwood News (Oct 20, 2025): TED revenue "rose above $100 million in 2023" (citing The Economist); YouTube search interest for "ted talk" peaked May 2017; of the 40 most-viewed videos on TED's official (main) channel only one was uploaded in the last five years. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/business/were-way-past-peak-ted-talk-new-vision-steward-sal-khan/)

### Inferences
- Annual event volume: TED's own figures conflict ("more than 3,000" in Feb 2025 materials and on NPR vs "more than 4000 ... annually" on the program page). The 2023 audited-style figure (3,300+) plus a directory showing 2,164 *upcoming* events in late October 2026 (most of which fall in the next ~6 months) is consistent with a 3,000–4,000/yr run-rate; a product team should plan for ~3,500 ±500 events/yr.
- Regional split (from the Dec-2025 directory pull, the only region data found): North America ≈ US 22% + Canada 3% ≈ 25%; India ≈ 14%; Europe is fragmented (Italy 5%, UK 4%, plus Türkiye 3% if counted) — likely 20–25% in aggregate but no official European total exists. India is the single largest non-US market.
- Uploads per year to the TEDx Talks channel: subtracting TED's rounded floors (200,000+ in Nov 2023; 250,000+ in Feb 2025) from the 272,588 count in Oct 2026 implies roughly 13,000–25,000 uploads/yr (the range is wide because TED's figures are rounded floors). TEDxBoulder's "40,000 submitted" claim is not corroborated by TED and may conflate submissions with publications, or be inflated.
- Mean views per video (~33.6k) will be far above the median because a small number of talks have tens of millions of views; the median is likely in the low thousands (see Q5 gaps — no median data was found).
- The directory count (2,164 upcoming) versus 1,365 listed for all of 2026 in Dec 2025 confirms that most events are posted to the directory only 2–6 months ahead; a discovery product must re-crawl continuously rather than rely on an annual snapshot.

### Gaps
- No official TED figure for events by region/country was found; the only country breakdown is a third-party directory pull (Dec 2025).
- No official "talks uploaded per year" figure was found for 2024–2026; vidIQ does not expose recent monthly upload counts and Social Blade could not be fetched.
- The official country count "180+" dates from Nov 2023; nothing more recent was found.

## Key Question 2: TEDx event types, licenses, attendee caps, speakers per event, recurrence

### Takeaway
TED grants a free, one-year, single-event, non-transferable license per city/community; 13 event types exist, and attendance is governed by four licensing tiers: Community (≤100 attendees, $150 ticket cap, default), Community Plus (>100, $250 cap, requires in-person attendance at a qualifying TED conference), Summit ($450 cap, after four 100+ events) and invitation-only Legacy (no cap). Typical programs run 90-minute sessions of ~4 full talks plus short pieces; real events found in this research feature 10–15 speakers.

### Cited Findings
- Event types listed by TED (13): Standard; University; Youth; ED; Salon (requires having organized at least one TEDx event with live speakers); TEDxPlay (limited-time license featuring curated TED Talks from Play@TED); TEDxLive (streams official TED conferences); TEDxWomen; Business (private, staff-only); Internal (select nonprofits/government, employee-only, naming exception); Library; Countdown (climate); Studio (produce talks "anytime and anywhere, independent of a TEDx event"; requires one prior live-speaker event). Each type has additional conditions tied to the licensee's tier. — [TED: TEDx event types](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/event-types)
- Licensing tiers: Community — "Up to 100 in-person attendees," $150 USD ticket cap, granted on license approval; Community Plus — more than 100 attendees, $250 cap, "You are eligible for this tier after attending a qualifying TED Conference"; Summit — $450 cap, "You are eligible for this tier if you have held at least four 100+ Standard, University, Youth, or ED events" with strong curation, allows two-day/longer events; Legacy — no ticket cap, "This is an invitation-only licensing tier granted to a small selection of long-standing organizers," "currently in pilot phase." Tier is assigned by TED at licensing; benefits don't transfer to team members or new licensees. — [TED: Licensing tiers](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/licensing-tiers)
- "Only individuals who have attended a qualifying TED conference in person may organize an event with more than 100 attendees." The qualifying list for 2026 is TED2026, TEDxWeekend Kuala Lumpur, TEDAI Vienna 2026 and TEDxWeekend Amazônia; for 2025: TED2025, TED Countdown Summit 2025, TEDAI Vienna 2025, TEDNext 2025; TED2027 already listed. Attendees must be 18+. — [TED: Qualifying conferences](https://www.ted.com/participate/organize-a-local-tedx-event/before-you-start/tedx-qualifying-conferences)
- Standard event: "up to 100 guests"; "A standard license lasts 12 months and covers one event"; after that the organizer renews or lets the license expire (releasing the name); event length "from a couple of hours to a full day"; Community-tier ticket cap $150; named after city/town/neighborhood (e.g., TEDxSydney, TEDxGracia). — [TED: Standard event](https://www.ted.com/participate/organize-a-local-tedx-event/before-you-start/event-types/standard-event)
- TEDx Rules (fetch returned a summary, not verbatim text): a license is one location-based series, valid one year, one city/one event; holder must live in the city and be the primary organizer; licenses cannot be transferred; TEDx events do not count as qualifying conferences; events are named after location, always with the full licensed name ("TEDx" + location as one word); "TEDx events cannot be co-branded with other organizations or integrated into other events or curricula"; TEDx "is a volunteer, not-for-profit endeavor"; ticket revenue must go to event costs; separate fundraisers/crowdfunding prohibited; sponsors may not have editorial control; press releases need approval via TEDxPR@ted.com; websites must carry footer text stating the event is operated under license from TED; watch parties may charge up to 50% of the ticket price. — [TED: TEDx Rules](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/tedx-rules)
- Naming rules: "TEDx" + location descriptor (city, neighborhood, street, square, lake, river with matching descriptor, e.g., TEDxBrick Lane); prohibited names: countries, states/provinces, regions, nicknames/historic names, buildings/museums, proprietary developments, organizations, themes/topics (e.g., "TEDxDesign"); "Major cities" are reserved for organizers whose events will draw 100+ attendees (i.e., who attended a qualifying conference); university events must include "School," "University," "College," or "U" and have institutional permission; a company name may be the main name only for internal events. — [TED: Naming your event](https://www.ted.com/pages/naming-your-event)
- Program structure guidance: "Most people create 90-minute sessions, each of which have four full-length talks and a few short presentations"; full-day events often have up to four sessions, half-day up to two; "Don't make a single talk session longer than 105 minutes"; "You aren't required to show pre-recorded TED or TEDx talks, but you are welcome to supplement your program with them"; every event opens with the official Chris Anderson video intro; no keynotes, panels, breakouts or Q&As. — [TED: Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)
- Real-world speaker counts: TEDxBoulder selects 12 speakers for a 2,000+ audience, has run every fall since 2010 (Community-Plus-or-above scale). — [TEDxBoulder About](https://tedxboulder.com/about); TEDxSurrey (Canada) had 12 speakers in Feb 2022 — [Surrey Now-Leader](https://www.surreynowleader.com/entertainment/5-surrey-talks-in-teds-global-video-spotlight-as-tedxsurrey-looks-for-more-valuable-and-timely-ideas/); TEDxLinz 2026 expects "10+ speakers and 300+ attendees" — [TEDxLinz speaker guide PDF](https://www.tedxlinz.at/_files/ugd/c88ea0_56ec8c5e1ea24496879bfe6ef88b919d.pdf); TEDx Bard College 2027 selected 15 speakers for 12 talks — [Bard CCE, Sep 3 2026](https://cce.bard.edu/news/tedx-bard-college-changing-the-narrative-for-2027-2026-09-03); TEDxWinnipeg runs annually in June (12th show in 2026) — [TEDxWinnipeg](https://tedxwinnipeg.ca/how-does-the-tedxwinnipeg-committee-choose-our-speakers/); TEDxTerry Talks (UBC) used a ~400-seat hall — [TEDxTerry](https://www.terry.ubc.ca/?p=16386).
- Speaker-prep calendar TED gives organizers: 6 months out thesis/outline due; 5 months script; 4 months second draft + first rehearsals; 3 months final draft; 2 months bi-weekly rehearsals; 1 month weekly; 2 weeks break; 1 week rehearsals; 1–2 days dress rehearsal. — [TED: Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)

### Inferences
- Because a license is single-event and must be renewed, most events are annual; Salon and Studio licenses are the recurring/between-event formats. The directory's type labels (Standard, Youth, Salon, University, TEDxPlay) are the practical taxonomy a product should filter on.
- The 100-attendee default means the majority of events are small (≤100 people) first- or second-year events; the larger "Community Plus/Summit/Legacy" events (TEDxBoulder, TEDxSydney-class) are the minority but have more featured-talk history and more applicant competition.
- With ~4 full talks per 90-minute session and 2–4 sessions, a typical full-day event has 8–16 full-length talk slots; the observed 10–15 speakers/event matches.

### Gaps
- TED publishes no official "average speakers per event" figure; the counts above are examples.
- The TEDx Rules fetch yielded a summary; exact wording of each numbered rule should be verified against the live page before quoting.
- No data on how many Community vs Community Plus vs Summit licenses exist.

## Key Question 3: How organizers find and select speakers (forms, fields, timelines, acceptance rates, criteria, rejection reasons)

### Takeaway
Selection is entirely local and volunteer-run: TED says teams "rely on research, local networks, and interviews more than pitch forms," tells applicants to use only an event's public form and to submit once, and warns that paid "TEDx services" are illegitimate. Public forms converge on the same fields (one-sentence idea, why it matters now, why you, prior-talk/pitch video, bio, links, local connection, commitment to coaching); windows typically open 6–9 months out and close 3–6 months before the event, with multi-round review (scoring, interviews/auditions) and mandatory coaching. The only hard numbers found: TEDxThirdWard 2026 received 786 applications and advanced 155 semifinalists; TEDx Bard 2027 chose 15 from 65+; TEDxBoulder interviews a top-30 and picks 12.

### Cited Findings
**TED's official guidance to prospective speakers**
- Speakers are selected by volunteer teams, not TED; "Only contact events that publish a public application form on their official TED.com event page or other official digital channels"; if no form is published, "respect that choice"; unsolicited pitches to volunteers on social media "are unlikely to get a good response." Teams "look for original, innovative work that the speaker is actively doing, not a personal story alone"; speakers "need not have international recognition"; teams "rely on research, local networks, and interviews more than pitch forms"; "Submit only one nomination per person per event." TEDx "does not allow speakers to pay in any form, including production costs, sponsor recruitment, ticket purchases, or coaching"; "Fee-based 'TEDx services' are not legitimate. Talks tied to paid arrangements become ineligible for publication"; sponsor representatives and event volunteers cannot speak at events they support; "Speaking at multiple events is rare. Duplicate talks won't be published, though new original work may be considered for another event"; report concerns to tedx@ted.com. (Fetch summary of the page.) — [TED: Speaking at TEDx](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)
- For the main TED conference: nominations accepted any time; "You can nominate yourself, someone you work for, or a client"; "It really only takes one suggestion"; TED talks are "typically 10 to 12 minutes" and the time limit is strictly enforced; for TEDx, "contact that event's organizer through its web page or social media." — [TED: Speaking at TED](https://www.ted.com/pages/speakingatted)
- Content guideline on serial applicants (verbatim): "TEDx discourages curating talks from speakers who are actively seeking participation in multiple TEDx events" within a short period; "Professional or circuit speakers, such as motivational speakers and life coaches, are not a fit for TEDx events." — [TED: TEDx Content Guidelines](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/tedx-content-guidelines)

**Real application forms (fields and timelines)**
- TEDxJacksonville (US) 2025 application for the Jan 2026 conference — Part I: full name, mailing address, email, phone (required), website/LinkedIn (optional). Part II (answers judged on "originality, significance, clarity, and depth," 100–250-word range): (1) "Your central idea in one sentence" (avoid "slogans or vague statements"); (2) a widely held belief your idea challenges and how; (3) why the idea matters now — plus a mandatory 1–2-minute video on YouTube/Vimeo/Dropbox/Drive; (4) why you are the right person (experience/insight/expertise); (5) have you presented the idea before and how it changed after feedback; (6) have you watched TED/TEDx talks on a similar topic and how yours differs; (7) one recent accomplishment (100-word max); (8) your connection to Jacksonville. Part III optional: 100–150-word bio, résumé/CV, portfolio, links to previous talks, headshot. Part IV: commitment (Yes / No / "I'd like to discuss further") to mandatory 1:1 and group coaching, virtual and in-person rehearsals, collaborative revision. Part V consent: talk will be recorded and posted; "TED would hold exclusive rights to distribute and edit the recording worldwide, in perpetuity"; TEDx is non-commercial; no commercial/political/religious agendas; signature/date. — [TEDxJacksonville application PDF](https://tedxjacksonville.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-speaker-application-for-Jan-2026-conference.pdf)
- TEDxWilson Park (Florence, Alabama, US; listing dated Mar 20, 2026): event tentatively Sep 4, 2026; theme "Ignite"; deadline Apr 10, 2026; talks capped at 12 minutes; fields: one-sentence idea summary; why the idea matters globally and/or locally; why you are the right person; supporting experience/credentials/research; optional past speaking video (author recommends a 1–3-minute pitch video); key question "Why are you the most qualified person in the world to speak on this subject?"; next step a phone interview before May 10, 2026. — [Speaker Accelerator: TEDxWilson Park](https://speakeraccelerator.substack.com/p/tedxwilson-park)
- TEDxBoulder (US) 2026 cycle: application deadline Sep 6, 2026; notification/confirmation Sep 12–16; training/kickoff Sep 22 (meet your coach); mandatory Tuesday practices 5–8 pm on Sep 29, Oct 6, 13, 20, 27, Nov 3, Nov 10; event Nov 14 (eTown Hall, "Roots & Wings"). Form: "a few brief questions" on what you want to share, why it matters to you, what keeps bringing you back to it, plus "a very brief video"; must live/work/have a meaningful connection to Boulder County; must hold all dates; "an idea, not a finished talk"; no PhD/platform needed; no selling. — [TEDxBoulder: Become a speaker](https://tedxboulder.com/become-a-speaker)
- TEDxBoulder process: form open most of the year; committee uses a "double blind process"; top 30 invited to in-person interview; 12 chosen; then coaching. — [TEDxBoulder About](https://tedxboulder.com/about)
- TEDxTCD 2027 (Trinity College Dublin, Ireland): event Mar 4, 2027; theme THRIVE; deadline Nov 6, 2026; fields: talk summary ≤200 words, bio ≤200 words, LinkedIn link, ~1-minute video outlining the talk; eligibility: Trinity community (students, alumni, staff, faculty, friends); talks 5–10 minutes; panel evaluation; outcome notified by Nov 30, 2026; coaching Jan–Feb 2027. — [TCD call for speakers](https://www.tcd.ie/ahss/news-and-events/events/tedx-tcd-2027-call-for-speakers/)
- TEDxTinHau Women (Hong Kong) 2026: deadline Mar 13, 2026; optional in-person auditions ~Mar 16 (no slides); decisions by end of April; talk development/rehearsals weekly Aug–Oct with professional coaches; event October; self- or third-party nomination via online form; criteria: ideas that challenge/educate, lineup diversity, compliance with TED content guidelines (no selling, politics, proselytizing incl. "new age," no non-scientific claims as science), time commitment. — [TEDxTinHau Women](https://tedxtinhauwomen.com/2026/03/03/choosing-tedxtinhauwomen-speakers/)
- TEDxWinnipeg (Canada): late October the lead team sets themes from world news; applications open December/January for a June event (≈5–6 months before); speaker lead passes ~6 suggestions to the speaker team; team also actively seeks people they know; applications ranked with a scoring card: originality, timeliness/relevance, speaker's connection to Winnipeg, fit with committee themes, lineup balance ("not too many work-related or medical-related talks"), representation of community voices. Reasons strong applications are declined: lineup balance; story lacks an angle "relatable to everyone"; no concrete action for the audience; applicant cannot commit the time; application focuses on wanting to be on stage rather than the idea; they receive "a lot of applications" and decline "many great applications"; reapplying is encouraged. — [TEDxWinnipeg](https://tedxwinnipeg.ca/how-does-the-tedxwinnipeg-committee-choose-our-speakers/)
- TEDxThirdWard 2026 (Houston, US; event Jun 14, 2026): 786 applications; 155 semifinalists; semifinalist emails sent Mar 2, 2026. — [Skool community post](https://www.skool.com/from-dream-to-speaker-stage-4093/for-those-applying-to-tedxthirdward)
- TEDx Bard College 2027 (US university; post Sep 3, 2026): "more than 65 applications" → interview process → 15 speakers / 12 talks for a May 2027 showcase. — [Bard CCE](https://cce.bard.edu/news/tedx-bard-college-changing-the-narrative-for-2027-2026-09-03)
- TEDxAuckland 2018 (NZ): "hundreds of detailed pitches" to the ideas email; plus direct outreach and referrals; informal coffee meetings; "No one was confirmed at the first meeting"; curation took "a few months"; selection "based on the same thing — their idea"; first pitch rarely the one selected. — [TEDxAuckland FAQ](https://tedxauckland.com/faq-what-is-the-speaker-curation-process/)
- TEDxDirigo (Maine, US): speakers sought "throughout the year," chosen by a curatorial team against the annual theme; wants local voices, novel inventions/designs/visions, fresh philosophical arguments, new historical perspectives, unusual discoveries, creative performance; does NOT want "sales pitches or fundraising asks," "the same talk you give everywhere else," "talks given on a whim," anything "pay-to-play." Explicitly disqualifying: "Barraging them with nominations or requests," "Mentioning other TED or TEDx talks you've given," "Promoting yourself as a sought-after keynote or motivational speaker," and "Signing up for courses or coaching that promise to help you land a TEDx talk." Post-selection: return contract and release immediately with 250-word bio and headshot; Speaker Launch; ≥4 hours 1:1 coaching; Speaker Camp; dress rehearsals; "60+ hours" total prep; talks mostly 6–12 minutes. — [TEDxDirigo: Speak](https://www.tedxdirigo.com/speak)
- TEDxLinz 2026 (Austria) speaker guide: four application routes — online form (tedxlinz.at/speaker), a 1:1 "discovery call" with the speaker curator (recording serves as application), a ≤3-minute pitch video emailed to the curator, or live audition events; pitch-recording fields: full name, email, phone, location, why you are the right person, what is controversial about the idea/common misconceptions/who would disagree; criteria in order: novelty, values, motivation, community impact (local and global), diversity; speakers not paid and may not pay; travel/accommodation possibly covered; coaching, dress rehearsal, speakers' dinner provided; event in English; no deadline stated; team reviews "in their free time." — [TEDxLinz speaker guide PDF](https://www.tedxlinz.at/_files/ugd/c88ea0_56ec8c5e1ea24496879bfe6ef88b919d.pdf)
- TEDxTerry Talks (UBC, Canada, 2015 cycle — dated example): fields: name, email, phone, faculty/department/program/year/student number, video URL (~1 minute, max 2) showing speaking style, written proposal (title + description, 300 words max), optional CV/essays; deadline Oct 13 for a Nov 7 event; selected notified Oct 15; rehearsals over the following three weeks; "exactly 18 minutes or less." — [TEDxTerry Talks](https://www.terry.ubc.ca/?p=16386)
- TEDxPittsburgh: receives "a large volume of requests," reviews every submission, cannot reply individually; "there are many examples when an application is referred to several months after the initial contact." — [TEDxPittsburgh](https://www.tedxpittsburgh.org/apply-to-speak)
- TEDxPortland organizer (Toastmasters magazine): "Ideas come first"; ordinary, unoriginal, half-baked ideas are rejected; stating you understand the TED platform and will accept coaching improves chances; check an event's history and "how many attendees its license allows," theme, production quality, past speakers; speakers aren't paid and may not be reimbursed for travel; the release lets TED distribute under Creative Commons. — [Toastmasters: How to Land a TED Talk](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/articles/how-to-land-a-ted-talk)
- Third-party timing claims (not measured): applications open "roughly 6 to 9 months before an event"; organizers plan "9 to 12 months ahead"; "the final 6 months before an event are devoted to speaker selection and coaching"; competition dips in holiday months. — [Speaker Accelerator Substack](https://speakeraccelerator.substack.com/p/i-pulled-every-published-tedx-event)
- TEDxNorthwesternU (US university) apply page: applications for 2024–25 closed; next cycle "Fall 2025"; disallowed content mirrors TED's guidelines (bad science, inflammatory politics/religion, promoting own/employer's products or books, copyright violations). — [TEDxNorthwesternU](https://www.tedxnorthwesternu.com/apply)

### Inferences
- Acceptance rates: the few data points imply first-round survival of ~20% (TEDxThirdWard 155/786) and final selection of ~1.5–2% at large open-call US events (if ~12–15 of 786), ~20–25% at a closed university community (Bard 15/65), and ~40% at the interview stage (Boulder 12/30). A product should set expectations of single-digit-percent odds for open-call city events and remind users that many events fill slots through research/referrals rather than forms.
- The "canonical" form the product should pre-fill for users: one-sentence idea; the belief it challenges; why now; why you (credentials/lived experience); 1–3-minute pitch or prior-talk video link; 100–250-word bio; local connection; links (LinkedIn/website); commitment to coaching/rehearsal dates; acknowledgement of content guidelines and exclusive video rights.
- Timeline model: open-call window closes ~3–6 months before the event (Winnipeg 5–6 months; TCD 4 months; TinHau 7 months; Boulder ~10 weeks); decision 2–8 weeks after close; then 6–12 weeks of mandatory coaching/rehearsals with fixed in-person dates.
- Explicit organizer hostility to "TEDx coaching courses" (Dirigo) and TED's "fee-based TEDx services are not legitimate" line mean a product must avoid positioning as a paid route to selection and should not encourage mentioning its use in applications.

### Gaps
- No organizer or TED-published aggregate acceptance-rate statistics exist; the three data points above are the only quantified ones found. Reddit/Quora threads were not reached before the search budget ran out.
- Form fields for India-, UK- and Canada-specific events in 2026 were not captured (the Canadian example is from 2015). The Smartsheet/Tally/Airtable forms surfaced in search could not be read (Smartsheet returned only metadata).

## Key Question 4: Official TEDx speaker rules (length, content guidelines, release/rights, no pay, slides, 2024–2026 updates incl. AI)

### Takeaway
Rules that bind every TEDx speaker: talks ≤18 minutes (TED calls 3–9 minutes "ideal"; up to 30 for interviews/dialogues, reviewed by TED); no payment in either direction; no selling, no partisan politics, no proselytizing, no bad science or health advice; every talk is recorded, fact-checked, and released under the TEDx Speaker Release, which grants TED exclusive distribution of the recording on its channels (one 2025 form says "exclusive rights to distribute and edit the recording worldwide, in perpetuity"); speakers get a link, not the video file; "TED reserves the right to remove, reject, annotate or limit the distribution of any TEDx talk." TED's organizer guide now carries AI-specific instructions: never use AI as a substitute for fact-checking, and don't use generative-AI tools with copyrighted or non-original input for slides.

### Cited Findings
**Length, payment, format (TEDx Rules; fetch returned a summary)**
- Talks are typically under 18 minutes, "with 3 to 9 minutes considered ideal"; longer formats up to 30 minutes may be allowed for interviews or two-speaker dialogues; talks over 18 minutes are reviewed by the TEDx team before publication; TEDx events may not pay speakers or charge them; event sponsors cannot be speakers; organizers cannot speak at events they contribute to; speakers cannot promote their own products, books or businesses, or their employer's; panels and audience Q&A are not permitted; all mainstage content must be recorded and all speakers/performers must sign the TEDx Speaker Release; videos go through the TED Media Uploader to the TEDx YouTube channel; "Videos may not be shared directly with speakers. Speakers should receive a link instead"; no text/logos/watermarks superimposed on talk content; slides shown full-frame; pre-roll/post-roll ≤10 seconds each; talks must comply with Content and Copyright Guidelines and organizers may withhold noncompliant talks but must inform TED and the speaker. — [TED: TEDx Rules](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/tedx-rules)
- TED main-stage talk length is "typically 10 to 12 minutes." — [TED: Speaking at TED](https://www.ted.com/pages/speakingatted)

**Content Guidelines (verbatim fragments as returned)**
- Organizers are "stewards of ideas worth spreading." Guideline 1 (commercial): "if it feels like an advertisement, it probably is"; speakers may mention their work when essential but may not pitch products/services, plug books, or ask for funding; no "branded logos, merchandise, calls to purchase or get involved with a business"; "Professional or circuit speakers, such as motivational speakers and life coaches, are not a fit for TEDx events"; "TEDx discourages curating talks from speakers who are actively seeking participation in multiple TEDx events." Guideline 2 (political): "TEDx stages are not the place for partisan politics, nor for extremist or inflammatory positions"; no campaigning for/against candidates, officials or bills; no pitching/recruiting for military, police or defense organizations. Guideline 3 (religious): no speakers trying to prove or persuade "the correctness of a single religion or belief system," including via "scientific proof"; "Be wary of new age beliefs." Guideline 4 (science): claims should be testable, peer-reviewed, consistent with established knowledge, presented by qualified speakers; terms like "quantum consciousness" or "crystal healing" are "a major red flag"; organizers must prevent misleading health/disease information. — [TED: TEDx Content Guidelines](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/tedx-content-guidelines)

**Fact-checking and AI**
- "Never use AI as a substitute for fact-checking"; treat AI "like a tertiary source, similar to Wikipedia"; acceptable uses: flagging claims needing sources, locating original references, summarizing a study's methods/limitations; "Don't use AI to approve scripts, speakers, or ideas against TEDx guidelines"; don't paste a full script into AI, don't share private information, don't assume its references are accurate. "TEDx talks should never be framed as health or medical advice"; replace "cure"/"heal" with "reduce risk of"; "if it can be checked, it should be checked!"; speakers should supply an annotated script with linked references; stories about other people need that person's permission; fact-checking takes "about 2–12 hours"; consequences: editor's note, removal from search results, or non-publication. — [TED: TEDx Fact-Checking FAQs](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/tedx-fact-checking-faqs)
- Slides: build at 16:9 (1920×1080 or 1280×720) or 4:3 (1024×768 or 800×600); "Use a common and simple sans serif font (like Inter, Helvetica or Verdana)"; minimal, one point per slide, no headline-and-bullets; speakers must own rights to all images and cite CC images; "Don't use generative AI tools with copyrighted material or input not original to the speaker." Speaker Release "before rehearsals begin"; TED "will request them if the talk is selected for wider sharing"; no podium/lectern unless needed; no pacing; avoid stripes/patterns/dangling jewelry; avoid "TED puns," "purely anecdotal evidence," utopianism/dystopianism. — [TED: Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)

**Release and rights**
- Speakers must sign the TEDx Speaker Release, "which allows exclusive distribution of the recorded talk on TED's digital channels"; some events add their own speaker agreement (rehearsals/prep); "No financial agreements are required." — [TED: Speaking at TEDx](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)
- Example consent text in a 2025 application: "TED would hold exclusive rights to distribute and edit the recording worldwide, in perpetuity." — [TEDxJacksonville application PDF](https://tedxjacksonville.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-speaker-application-for-Jan-2026-conference.pdf)
- Publishing Policy (verbatim): "TED reserves the right to remove, reject, annotate or limit the distribution of any TEDx talk"; "all speakers have signed a speaker release agreeing to TED publishing their talk on the TEDx YouTube channel," but if a speaker no longer wants it online they contact tedx@ted.com and TED "will take steps to remove the talk"; talks later disproven get an editor's note and reduced search visibility; talks with uncleared copyrighted material may be removed or never published; organizers' compliance "may affect their license status." — [TED: TEDx Publishing Policy](https://www.ted.com/tedx/tedx-publishing-policy)
- TED Talks Usage Policy (verbatim): talks are shared under "CC BY–NC–ND 4.0 International"; "NC: means you cannot use TED Talks in any commercial context or to gain any type of revenue, payment or fee"; "ND: means that no derivative works are permitted so you cannot edit, remix, create, modify or alter the form of the TED Talks"; "You must attribute TED as the owner of the TED Talk and add a visible link back to TED.com"; "You must use the TED embeddable player"; the page does not say explicitly whether TEDx talks are covered. — [TED Talks Usage Policy](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/ted-talks/ted-talks-usage-policy)
- Terms of Use (updated May 7, 2024): published content is under "Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–NoDerivatives 4.0 International license (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)"; "Any use beyond the scope of the applicable license or Usage Policy requires TED's prior written authorization." — [TED.com Terms of Use](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-com-terms-of-use)
- Upload rules: titles ≤100 characters including speaker and event name; descriptions ≤5,000 characters including bio; uploads within one month of the event and required before license renewal; review typically 3–5 business days; opening reel ≤10 s; sponsor logos only at beginning/end; no superimposed text/subtitles/labels; slides full-frame; optional sign-language version as second asset. — [TED: Upload your videos](https://www.ted.com/participate/organize-a-local-tedx-event/tedx-organizer-guide/video-photography/video/upload-your-videos)
- Enforcement of the no-pay rule: a Dubai Marina TEDx event scheduled for Oct 16 was cancelled and the organiser's licence revoked after speakers were asked to pay up to $25,000 (one quoted $15,000 for a "premium slot," paid via PayPal/Stripe); TED opened an internal investigation; organisers apologised and began refunds. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-tedx-event-cancelled-alleged-25000-speaker-fees) (article date not captured in fetch)

### Inferences
- The combination of "exclusive distribution," "speakers receive a link, not the file," and CC BY-NC-ND means a speaker cannot legitimately re-upload their talk to their own channel, re-edit it, or monetize it; permitted promotion is sharing/embedding the official YouTube/TED link (see Q5).
- "TEDx speaker agreement changes" (asked about): no dated changelog was found; the AI language in the fact-checking FAQ and slide guidance, and the May-2024 Terms of Use revision, are the only identifiable 2024–2026 updates. Treat the AI guidance as current but of unknown exact date.
- For a product aimed at coaches/consultants/authors: the content guidelines' explicit exclusion of "motivational speakers and life coaches" and of book-plugging, plus the rule against seeking multiple TEDx slots, directly target this user base; the product must coach users toward a single idea-first talk, not a brand platform.

### Gaps
- The full verbatim text of the TEDx Speaker Release was not retrieved (TED hosts it behind the organizer rules page; no public copy was fetched). The "exclusive ... worldwide, in perpetuity" wording is from one organizer's form, not TED's master document.
- The TEDx Rules page fetch produced a paraphrase; exact numbered-rule wording should be re-verified.
- No dated TED announcement of rule changes in 2024–2026 was found; the organizer-guide pages carry no dates.

## Key Question 5: How a TEDx talk gets onto TED.com, share featured, YouTube view distribution, promotion rules

### Takeaway
TED's editorial/curation team (Head of Media and Curation Helen Walters) picks a tiny fraction of TEDx uploads for TED.com or the main TED YouTube channel — about 1% historically (122 of 12,900 by Jan 2012) and "less than 0.1%" per one large organizer today; no official current rate exists. Views on the TEDx Talks channel are heavily skewed (mean ≈33.6k; a handful exceed millions), and TED's own guidance credits headline/description craft and personal/network outreach, not paid promotion. Speakers may share the official link and call themselves a TEDx speaker in practice, but may not receive or re-upload the file, use the TED logo, or imply TED endorsement.

### Cited Findings
- Historical featured rate: 122 TEDx talks featured on TED.com out of 12,900 in the library by Feb 2012 (~0.95%); 22 featured in Jan 2012 against 785 uploads (~2.8%). — [TED Blog, Feb 2012](https://blog.ted.com/state-of-the-x-stats-on-tedx-and-tedxtalks-in-january/)
- TEDxBoulder: 8 of its talks featured on TED.com; claims "less than a 0.1% selection rate" for roughly 40,000 TEDx videos submitted to TED each year. — [TEDxBoulder About](https://tedxboulder.com/about)
- TEDxSurrey: 5 of 12 talks from its Feb 2022 event were selected by TED as "valuable and timely ideas for our global audience" and showcased on TED's main YouTube channel (34M subscribers), not TED.com; one ("The four phases of retirement," Dr. Riley Moynes) passed 1 million views within ~3 months. — [Surrey Now-Leader, Aug 2022](https://www.surreynowleader.com/entertainment/5-surrey-talks-in-teds-global-video-spotlight-as-tedxsurrey-looks-for-more-valuable-and-timely-ideas/)
- Organizers must keep signed releases because TED "will request them if the talk is selected for wider sharing." — [TED: Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)
- TED's Oct 2025 announcement names Helen Walters as head of media and curation and says an "Ideas Council" will be recruited to work with her team. — [TED Blog, Oct 15 2025](https://blog.ted.com/?p=118404)
- Channel totals: 272,588 videos, 9.16B views, mean ≈33,600 views/video (Oct 2026). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@tedx/)
- Two of the ten most-viewed TED talks were TEDx talks by previously unknown speakers (TEDxPortland organizer). — [Toastmasters](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/articles/how-to-land-a-ted-talk)
- TED's "Get views" guidance: headlines should "Use words that someone looking for a talk on the topic would enter in their search engine"; descriptions open with 2–3 hook sentences; 4–7-sentence bios; personal outreach ("some speakers have reached hundreds of thousands of views this way"); targeted outreach to bloggers/influencers/subreddits; send on Monday/Tuesday at lunch or evening, avoid weekends; TED-Ed "flips"; uploads can take up to 3 days to approve so upload before promoting. — [TED: Get views](https://www.ted.com/pages/get-views)
- Promotion & press rules for organizers: "it is incredibly important to us that you make clear that your event is a TEDx event, and is independently organized"; "Never use the TED logo, and don't allow journalists to use it"; never say "organized by TED," "sponsored by TED," "official TED event," or "TED is coming to [city]"; all press releases go through tedxpr@ted.com (media liaison Melody Serafino); press may not film talks; teaser trailers encouraged. — [TED: Promotion and press](https://www.ted.com/pages/promotion-and-press)
- Logo rules: only the event's unique "TEDx + EventName" logo may be used; "The TED and TEDx logos must never be used" generically; the "x = independently organized TED event" tagline is no longer required; TED Red is HEX #EB0028 (Pantone 185); Helvetica or Inter Light; white or black backgrounds only; no lock-ups with other logos. — [TED: Your TEDx logo](https://www.ted.com/pages/your-tedx-logo)
- Speaker-side promotion tactics (Entrepreneur, Jun 26 2019): repost the talk several times with new angles ("you're going to have to post that talk more than once"); Instagram Story quizzes; newsletter inclusion; "nth viewer" contests; email signature link; the author's talk passed 500 views in its first week. — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/335776)
- Non-TEDx evidence on YouTube dynamics: a Royal Institution talk got ~90% of its views from YouTube's homepage/suggested lists rather than sharing; averaged ~100 views/day for months until YouTube featured it, after which it gained more views in days than in its first year; a commenter reports TED talks gain ~500k extra views/day while on TED's front page. — [Kucharski Substack, Jul 2023](https://kucharski.substack.com/p/going-viral)
- Sherwood (Oct 2025): on TED's main channel only 1 of the 40 most-viewed videos was uploaded in the last five years; "ted talk" search interest peaked May 2017. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/business/were-way-past-peak-ted-talk-new-vision-steward-sal-khan/)
- Sharing rules: "You must use the TED embeddable player"; no commercial use, no derivatives, attribution with link to TED.com. — [TED Talks Usage Policy](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/ted-talks/ted-talks-usage-policy)
- Speakers receive a link rather than the file: "Videos may not be shared directly with speakers. Speakers should receive a link instead." (summary) — [TED: TEDx Rules](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/tedx-rules)

### Inferences
- Featured-on-TED.com odds are on the order of 0.1–1%; the product should treat TED.com selection as a lottery and focus post-talk strategy on the TEDx Talks YouTube page (title/description/bio quality set by the organizer at upload — a speaker should supply these assets to the organizer before upload, since titles are capped at 100 characters and descriptions at 5,000).
- The "first 48 hours" factor was NOT found in any TED source; TED's own advice is to time outreach to weekdays and to re-promote repeatedly. The only timing evidence (non-TEDx) says algorithmic suggestion, not launch-day sharing, drives large view counts.
- Speakers may describe themselves as "TEDx speaker" in bios (ubiquitous practice; no TED rule found prohibiting it), but may not use the TED logo, the generic TEDx logo, or imply TED endorsement; re-uploading the full talk to a personal channel conflicts with the exclusive release and ND license.

### Gaps
- No median or percentile distribution of TEDx talk views was found (no dataset reached); the mean is derivable but misleading.
- No official TED statement of the current share of TEDx talks selected for TED.com; nothing found on the selection workflow beyond "TED's curation team."
- No TED rule found that explicitly addresses whether speakers may use their event's TEDx logo or the "TEDx speaker" phrase in self-promotion; the License Agreement (Q7) restricts anyone "that organizes, participates in, or performs services regarding your TEDx event" from using the marks beyond the event, which arguably covers speakers.

## Key Question 6: TED's official event directory, API/data, terms on scraping, alternative discovery sources

### Takeaway
ted.com/tedx/events is a paginated list (50/page, "?page=N"), keyword-searchable, with Upcoming/Past, date and webcast filters; each listing shows date, name, city/country, availability status and event type, linking to /tedx/events/{5-digit id}. There is no event API or feed (only an RSS feed for talks), and the May-2024 Terms of Use prohibit automated scraping of "TED Content" absent a written license. TED tells speakers the directory is where to find events and to apply only via forms published on the event's TED.com page or official channels; third-party newsletters (e.g., Speaker Accelerator) already aggregate open calls manually.

### Cited Findings
- Directory structure (fetched Oct 30, 2026): keyword box "Search TEDx events"; "Upcoming"/"Past" toggle; date dropdown ("Any date"); "Webcast" filter; listings grouped under "Today," "Tomorrow," or week headings; fields per listing: date, event name (link), city and country, status ("Spaces available," "Sold out," "Invite only," "Happening now"), event type ("Standard," "Youth," "Salon," "University," "TEDxPlay"); theme, website and "apply to speak" links are NOT shown in listings; URL pattern `https://www.ted.com/tedx/events/{id}` with five-digit IDs (examples: TEDxInnsbruck 64252, TEDxHaarlem 68044, TEDxColombo 65156, TEDxDehong School Beijing Youth 67835, TEDxIsfahan University of Technology Salon 68345, TEDxNOLA 65842, TEDxIIT Kharagpur Play 71391); "2,164 upcoming events," 50 per page, pages 1–44 via `?page=N`; no map view; no API/JSON feed; the only feed in metadata is `https://www.ted.com/feeds/talks.rss` (talks, not events). — [TED TEDx events directory](https://www.ted.com/tedx/events)
- TED's advice to speakers: find events on the TEDx events page; "Only contact events that publish a public application form on their official TED.com event page or other official digital channels." — [TED: Speaking at TEDx](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)
- Terms of Use, Section 6.4 (verbatim, date updated May 7, 2024): "You may not use automated means — including scraping tools, bots, crawlers, harvesting technologies, or similar mechanisms — to access, collect, download, extract, or analyze TED Content in a manner inconsistent with these Terms"; scraping or AI use is "prohibited absent a separate written license agreement with TED establishing safeguards." Section 6: "you agree not to publish, distribute, reproduce, transmit, display, host, modify, or otherwise make TED Content available ... without TED's prior written authorization." Educational use excludes "incorporating TED Content into research datasets, archives, or collections." — [TED.com Terms of Use](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-com-terms-of-use)
- Third-party aggregation already exists: a Substack ("Speaker Accelerator," Geleen Antonio) pulled the full 2026 list from the TEDx events map and publishes per-event open-call listings (e.g., TEDxWilson Park with deadline, fields and interview step) and sells "1:1 Application Strategy Session[s]." — [Speaker Accelerator: 2026 pull](https://speakeraccelerator.substack.com/p/i-pulled-every-published-tedx-event); [Speaker Accelerator: TEDxWilson Park](https://speakeraccelerator.substack.com/p/tedxwilson-park)
- A Skool community ("From Dream to Speaker Stage," 343 members) relays TEDx calls and semifinalist news. — [Skool](https://www.skool.com/from-dream-to-speaker-stage-4093/for-those-applying-to-tedxthirdward)
- Organizer-side channels observed in this research: event websites on Wix/WordPress with PDF or embedded forms (TEDxJacksonville PDF; TEDxBoulder; TEDxPittsburgh; TEDxNorthwesternU; TEDxDirigo; TEDxLinz with form + discovery-call booking + email pitch), university news pages (Bard, Trinity College Dublin, and — per search-result titles only — Penn State Behrend, UGA, Mississippi State, Texas State), and local newspapers (Surrey Now-Leader; Spokane U-District per search titles). — see individual citations in Q3.
- Organizers must blog "at least once or twice per week" and email guests regularly; event photos must be posted to Flickr under Creative Commons; social handles must mirror the licensed name. — [TED: Promotion and press](https://www.ted.com/pages/promotion-and-press); [TED: TEDx Rules (summary)](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/tedx-rules)
- TEDxPortland organizer: check at ted.com/tedx/events "how long the event has run and how many attendees its license allows." — [Toastmasters](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/articles/how-to-land-a-ted-talk)

### Inferences
- Legally safest data strategy: do not scrape ted.com; instead (a) have users/organizers submit event pages, (b) deep-link to `ted.com/tedx/events/{id}` for canonical details, (c) ingest organizer-owned sites, social posts and newsletters (not "TED Content"), and (d) seek a written data license from TED (the Terms explicitly contemplate one).
- The directory is the only canonical source of license status (type, tier-implied size via "Invite only"/"Spaces available," dates); "apply to speak" information lives only on organizer sites, so a product must crawl ~2,000 heterogeneous organizer domains or crowdsource.
- Status labels ("Invite only") and type labels are useful to rank events for applicants (open-call likelihood is highest for Standard/University events with "Spaces available").

### Gaps
- Individual event pages (/tedx/events/{id}) were not fetched; the exact field set per event page (theme, website link, organizer, venue, "apply" link) is unverified.
- No evidence was found of TED ever granting a third-party data license for the events directory, nor of enforcement against scrapers.
- Eventbrite/Instagram/LinkedIn as discovery channels were not searched before the budget ran out.

## Key Question 7: TED/TEDx trademark and brand-use rules for third parties; "TEDx OS" risk; how comparable services name themselves

### Takeaway
TED's License Agreement prohibits use of its marks "in connection with any work or service purporting to offer guidance on how to give an effective TED Talk or TEDx Talk, or how to get chosen to deliver a TED Talk or TEDx Talk," and allows public-speaking guidance only if it "shall not in any way refer to TED"; TED's Terms say third parties may not "publish, display, distribute or use these marks ... for any purposes without prior written authorization"; TED labels fee-based "TEDx services" illegitimate and organizers treat enrolment in "land a TEDx talk" courses as disqualifying. Section 2: "All Event Names will be owned exclusively by TED and are licensed to you according to these terms and conditions." - TED's own speaker page says talks "tied to paid arrangements become ineligible for publication," which is a consequence that falls on the speaker, not only the vendor. — [TED: Speaking at TEDx](https://www.ted.com/about/programs-initiatives/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)

### Inferences
- "TEDx OS" risk assessment: (1) the name incorporates TED's registered mark as the dominant element, in a domain/app context TED's Terms expressly reserve; (2) the service — helping people find, apply to, get selected for and deliver TEDx talks — is the exact category the License Agreement forbids licensees and their contractors from attaching the marks to, and TED's stated policy calls fee-based TEDx services illegitimate; (3) TED has a track record of C&D letters even against parody and of license revocation for pay-to-speak; (4) organizers may penalize applicants who use such services, so branding with TEDx could harm users' odds. Trademark fair-use (nominative/descriptive) arguments exist for saying "helps you apply to TEDx events" in copy, but not for using TEDx as the product name. Recommend a TED-free brand (as competitors do), a prominent "not affiliated with or endorsed by TED Conferences LLC; TEDx is a trademark of TED" disclaimer, no red "TEDx" typography or #EB0028 red/Helvetica trade dress, and avoiding any promise of "getting selected."
- Business-model risk: a paid product that touches the application should be positioned as general idea-development/public-speaking coaching ("services that offer guidance on public speaking") and event discovery, not "TEDx coaching," and must never route money to organizers or imply slots can be bought.

## Key Question 8: TED's organizational changes 2024–2026 and implications for TEDx

### Takeaway
Chris Anderson announced on Feb 4, 2025 that he would hand TED to new stewards; after a nine-month LionTree-run search with "nearly 100" interested groups (including outright purchase offers and "nine-figure checks"), TED announced on Oct 15, 2025 that it stays a nonprofit, Sal Khan joins the board as unpaid "Vision Steward," Logan McClure Davda (co-founder of TED Fellows, formerly Head of Impact) becomes CEO, Jay Herratti (former head of TEDx) moves to the board, and Anderson formally handed over at TED2026 in Vancouver (Apr 13–17, 2026) — TED's last Vancouver edition before a 2027 return to California. No changes to the TEDx program, licensing, pricing or rulebook were announced; Anderson explicitly cited TEDx's volunteer culture as a reason to reject a for-profit route.

### Cited Findings
- Feb 4, 2025: Anderson, curator for 25 years, said he is stepping aside; open invitation to anyone "with the resources and ideas"; LionTree Advisors retained; criteria: global reach, editorial independence, inclusivity, significant new funding ("How large of a capital commitment can you make to TED's future?"); contact nextchapter@ted.com; Monique Ruff-Bell (chief program and strategy officer): TED seeks the best vision, "not the highest bidder." — [Skift Meetings, Feb 4 2025](https://meetings.skift.com/2025/02/04/chris-anderson-stepping-down-as-the-head-of-ted/)
