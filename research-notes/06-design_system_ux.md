## Key Question 1: Typography — which typefaces, pairings, licensing, variable support, and type scales for a dashboard-heavy app plus a long-form script editor and teleprompter

### Takeaway
The 2026 premium-SaaS sans default is a neutral variable grotesk (Inter, Geist, Plus Jakarta Sans, Manrope, General Sans/Satoshi), all free; "editorial authority" comes from pairing it with an open-source optical-size serif (Newsreader or Source Serif 4 for text, Fraunces or Instrument Serif for display). Commercial faces such as Söhne/Tiempos (Klim) are one-off perpetual licences priced by page views/MAUs and explicitly do not cover embedding in apps without contacting the foundry. For long-form editing keep lines at ~50–75 characters and line-height ≥1.5; for teleprompters prioritise large type, a narrow centred column and 100–150 wpm scroll.

### Cited Findings

**Sans-serif candidates (character, licensing, variable support)**
- Inter: designed by Rasmus Andersson, free under the SIL Open Font License; Geist: Vercel's open-source font, free under the OFL and "ships in variable form", with Geist Mono as its matching monospace; Plus Jakarta Sans, Manrope, DM Sans, Space Grotesk, Sora, Outfit, Work Sans all listed as free under the OFL (Manrope confirmed variable) — [Made Good Designs, 10 Best Tech & Startup Fonts (2026)](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=8122)
- Comparison of Inter vs Geist vs Plus Jakarta Sans for B2B SaaS: Inter is "neutral and highly legible" but "its ubiquity makes it look competent but not distinctive", best for dense product UI and data tables (tabular numerals, stylistic set 03 for IDs), weights 100–900; Geist is "confident and technical, with sharper terminals and tighter apertures", best for marketing heroes and developer tools, but "small body text on Windows ClearType feels cramped" (one small in-office test showed ~8% slower reading at 16px); Plus Jakarta Sans is the warmest/humanist, weights 200–800, better for education/HR buyers and "less suited to very technical tools". All three are OFL, all shipped as variable fonts in 2026, and all are within ~10 KB of each other as WOFF2 — [Pravin Kumar, Inter vs Geist vs Plus Jakarta Sans for B2B (2026)](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/inter-geist-plus-jakarta-sans-webflow-b2b-2026)
- Same source's weight/tracking advice: use one variable family across hero, body and UI; create contrast through weight and tracking (hero 700 with negative tracking, body 400 normal tracking, UI labels 500 with positive tracking); load body with `font-display: swap` and the hero headline with `block` and a 100 ms timeout; it advises against adding a second display font for most B2B SaaS sites — [Pravin Kumar](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/inter-geist-plus-jakarta-sans-webflow-b2b-2026)
- Vercel's own design guidance: Geist Sans for prose, headings, labels, controls, tables and figures; Geist Mono only for code, commands, paths, raw tokens, timestamps and short identifiers; Google Fonts loads Geist and Geist Mono at weights 400–600. Its type tokens are named display, page-title, title, section, subsection, lede, body, compact, label, metadata, with weights regular/heading/medium/semibold (numeric values not exposed) — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)
- A SaaS font ranking lists Geist (variable, 9 weights, 114 KB) as the top heading pick, Inter ("tall x-height designed for screen UI", tabular figures, variable, 525 KB) and Plus Jakarta Sans ("suited to SaaS product interfaces"); it notes Notion pairs Inter with a custom serif for content and Stripe uses a custom typeface, Camphor — [Font Alternatives, Best Fonts for SaaS](https://fontalternatives.com/best-fonts-for/saas/)
- Fontshare (Indian Type Foundry) fonts are "100% free for personal and commercial use" and usable "in any media, at any scale, and in any location worldwide", but "are not open-source and are still controlled by an ITF EULA" (so webfont embedding/modification terms are governed by that EULA, not the OFL) — [Indian Type Foundry, Introducing Fontshare](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/news/introducing-fontshare); a secondary guide lists General Sans as "Free for personal/limited" and says to confirm Fontshare terms before commercial use — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=8122). The primary ITF statement is the more authoritative of the two.
- Caution on a popular listicle: it links Satoshi to a non-official download site (dafontfree.io) and attributes General Sans to "Steve Matteson in 1998", which contradicts ITF's authorship via Fontshare; treat that page as unreliable for provenance — [Mockuuups, 30 Best Modern Fonts for Web and Apps (2026)](https://mockuuups.studio/blog/post/best-fonts-for-apps/) vs [ITF](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/news/introducing-fontshare)

**Serif candidates for "thought-leadership/editorial" voice**
- Fraunces is "a display, 'Old Style' soft-serif typeface inspired by the mannerisms of early 20th century typefaces such as Windsor, Souvenir, and the Cooper Seri[f]" (served via Google Fonts) — [Google Fonts, Fraunces](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fraunces)
- Instrument Serif "is a condensed display font designed for the Instrument brand" (served via Google Fonts) — [Google Fonts, Instrument Serif](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Instrument+Serif)
- Newsreader, by Production Type, is "primarily intended for continuous on-screen reading in content-rich environments" such as news sites — [Google Fonts, Newsreader](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Newsreader)
- Source Serif 4 is "a serif typeface in the transitional style" "designed to complement the Source Sans Pro family" — [Google Fonts, Source Serif 4](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Source+Serif+4)
- TED's own typography is Neue Helvetica, chosen for neutrality that "keeps the focus on speakers' ideas rather than on the design itself" and for broad language support; it has been used by TED conferences "since the beginning" and came to ted.com in the 2015 redesign — [Monotype case study](https://www.monotype.com/resources/case-studies/neue-helvetica-comes-into-its-own-for-ted). TED's TEDx logo guidance says: "Use Helvetica. If you don't have it, use Inter Light" and "Don't use any other typeface" — [TED, Your TEDx Logo](https://www.ted.com/pages/your-tedx-logo). (Relevant to the brand-confusion question: Helvetica/Inter Light + red is literally TED's prescribed look.)

**Commercial foundry licensing (Klim: Söhne, Tiempos, Signifier)**
- Klim: "All fonts can be used worldwide, all fees are one-off, and all licences have no expiry"; licence categories are Desktop, Web, App, Advertising, Broadcasting, OEM, Test, with use cases including "Web application", "Mobile application", "Desktop application", "Unlimited application" and "Enterprise" — [Klim, Licences](https://klim.co.nz/licences/)
- Klim's FAQ references web tiers tied to "20,000 monthly page views" or "5,000 monthly unique users", app thresholds of "2,500 MAUs" or "2,500 downloads", and a minimum desktop licence of 5 users; no prices are published on the FAQ — [Klim FAQs](https://klim.co.nz/faqs/)
- Klim Web Font Licence: usage measured as monthly page views/unique users averaged over three consecutive months; must cover total traffic across all websites; only Klim-supplied WOFF2 may be served; subsetting allowed only to reduce file size; "Embedding the fonts in software, hardware, digital documents, apps, or devices is prohibited without contacting Klim"; letting remote users edit text to create new documents is not allowed without contacting Klim; the agreement does not address SaaS behind login — [Klim Web Font Licence Agreement](https://klim.co.nz/license/0)

**Readability research for long-form editors**
- Print tradition: 45–75 characters per line (cpl) with "the ideal is 66 cpl (including letters and spaces)" (Bringhurst); on screen Dyson & Haselgrove (2001) proposed ~55 cpl as the best compromise between speed and comprehension; Dyson & Kipping (1998) found 100 cpl read faster than 25 cpl with equal comprehension; Ling & Van Schaik (2006): longer lines suit scanning, shorter lines suit thorough reading; CSS `max-width: 66ch` fixes the measure regardless of text size — [Wikipedia, Line length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_length)
- Practitioner synthesis: desktop 45–75 cpl, tablet 40–65, mobile 30–50; minimum body 16px desktop / 15px tablet / 14–15px mobile; line-height baseline 1.5, 1.6–1.7 for lines over 75 cpl; WCAG 1.4.8 recommends max 80 cpl (40 for CJK); test at 200% zoom per WCAG 1.4.4; left-align rather than justify; offer user controls for font size, line spacing and background; British Dyslexia Association recommends 60–70 cpl for dyslexic readers — [UXPin, Optimal Line Length](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/optimal-line-length-for-readability/)
- Vercel: "Keep prose near 60 to 68 characters per line. Avoid tiny muted text." — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)

**Teleprompter display and speaking rate**
- Speaking rates: 100–125 wpm for emotional/serious material, 125–150 wpm for most professional videos ("most people speak at around 125–150 words per minute"), 150–170 wpm for high-energy short-form; start the scroll 5–10% slower than feels right; calibrate by timing one paragraph read aloud; a five-minute delivery is roughly 625–750 words; keep most lines to 1–2 sentences; use delivery cues like (pause); use a highlight line guide — [Teleprompter.com, Script speed tips](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/script-speed-tips-for-teleprompter-delivery)
- Teleprompter settings: font "large enough to read from arm's length" and large enough to read with peripheral vision; start 120–150 wpm (fast speakers 160–180, deliberate speakers 100–120); narrow the text column "so your eyes don't travel side to side"; keep text centred; short paragraphs of 2–3 sentences; choose high-contrast text/background; mirror mode exists for beam-splitter hardware; place the text as close to the lens as possible and position the window within inches of the webcam; glance at keywords rather than reading every word — [Speakflow, Free Online Teleprompter Guide (2026)](https://www.speakflow.com/blog/posts/free-online-teleprompter-guide)

### Inferences
- Recommended pairings (all free/OFL unless noted):
  1. **"Authority editorial" (recommended default):** Newsreader (text serif with optical-size axis) for script-editor body and long-form reading + Inter or Geist for UI chrome + Geist Mono/JetBrains Mono for timestamps and metrics. Rationale: Newsreader is explicitly built for continuous on-screen reading; Inter/Geist carry tabular numerals and dense-UI legibility. Cost: $0.
  2. **"Stage display":** Fraunces (display, soft old-style; Google Fonts) or Instrument Serif (condensed display; Google Fonts) for H1/hero/quotes only, General Sans or Plus Jakarta Sans (warmer humanist sans) for UI and body. Fraunces/Instrument Serif are display faces and should not be used below ~24px. Cost: $0 (General Sans under ITF EULA, not OFL — verify EULA for app embedding).
  3. **"Premium commercial":** Söhne (UI) + Tiempos Text (editor) from Klim. Cost: one-off perpetual licences priced by page views/MAUs; app embedding and SaaS-behind-login use require contacting Klim. Budget risk and licence-tier audits make this the lowest-priority option; choose only if the brand needs a bespoke feel.
- Avoid Helvetica/Neue Helvetica and Inter Light as the primary brand face in a red-accented UI, because TED's own guidelines prescribe exactly that combination (see Key Question 9).
- Proposed type scale (dashboard, base 16px, rem units; names mirror Vercel's token vocabulary):
  - display 40/44px, weight 600, letter-spacing −0.02em
  - page-title 28/36, 600, −0.015em
  - title 22/28, 600, −0.01em
  - section 18/26, 600
  - lede 18/28, 400 (serif allowed)
  - body 16/24, 400
  - compact 14/20, 400 (tables, dense cards)
  - label 13/18, 500, +0.01em
  - metadata 12/16, 500, +0.02em — never rendered in a colour under 4.5:1 ("avoid tiny muted text").
- Proposed editor (script) measure: body 19–20px Newsreader (opsz ≈ text), line-height 1.6, `max-width: 66ch` (~640–700px), left-aligned, with user controls for size/line-height/background and a "focus" mode that hides chrome. Word-count and estimated spoken duration at 130 wpm shown in a persistent footer (teleprompter.com's 125–150 wpm range).
- Proposed teleprompter defaults: 48–64px sans at 1.3–1.4 line-height on a near-black background with ivory text (#F4EFE6 on #000 = 18.3:1, computed), a centred column of ~30–40 characters, eye-line marker in the upper third, scroll speed set in wpm (default 130, range 90–180), keyboard shortcuts for ±5% speed, pause, and restart; optional mirror toggle. The default rate is derived from the two teleprompter guides' 120–150 wpm bands.

## Key Question 2: Color — systems used by premium apps, building a WCAG 2.2 AA semantic token system, candidate "stage/spotlight/authority" palettes, and TED's red

### Takeaway
Premium 2025–26 product UIs are built on near-monochrome neutral scales where colour is reserved for state, action and data (Vercel), implemented as 10–12-step scales with fixed step roles (Vercel Geist 100–1000; Radix 1–12) and remapped per theme. TED's red is documented by TED itself as #EB0028 (Pantone 185) on its TEDx logo page, while the widely circulated #E62B1E (Pantone 485) appears on third-party logo sites; both are near-pure vivid reds that are also borderline for AA white text (4.4–4.6:1), so a non-affiliated product should avoid any vivid red + black + Helvetica/Inter combination and signal "stage" with ink navy + warm ivory + a single gold/amber or electric accent instead.

### Cited Findings

**How premium systems structure colour**
- Vercel's design guidance: "the design is monochrome. Color is used only for state, action, or data meaning, and always paired with a non-color cue"; semantic tokens are surface-primary/secondary/contrast, text-primary/secondary/on-contrast, border-subtle/default/strong/on-contrast, focus, info/success/warning/error, and chart-1…6; "Target WCAG AA. Never rely on color alone"; respect reduced-motion; use visible focus; 12-column desktop / 6 tablet / 4 mobile grid; radii "restrained" — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)
- Vercel Geist colour scales (backgrounds, gray, gray-alpha, blue, red, amber, green, teal, purple, pink) use steps 100–1000 with fixed roles: 100 default background, 200 hover background, 300 active background, 400 default border, 500 hover border, 600 active border, 700 high-contrast background, 800 hover high-contrast background, 900 secondary text/icons, 1000 primary text/icons; two page backgrounds (`--ds-background-100` default, `--ds-background-200` "secondary background to use sparingly"); gray-alpha only where the background should show through; text/icon steps are "designed for accessible text and icons"; P3 colours used on supported displays — [Vercel Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)
- Radix Colors 12-step roles: 1 app background, 2 subtle background (cards, sidebars, code), 3 UI element background, 4 hovered, 5 active/selected, 6 subtle border, 7 element border, 8 hovered border + focus rings, 9 solid background (highest chroma), 10 hovered solid, 11 low-contrast text, 12 high-contrast text; steps 11 and 12 are guaranteed APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90 on a step-2 background; most step-9 solids are designed for white text, but Sky, Mint, Lime, Yellow and Amber are designed for dark text; for dark mode use step 1 or 2 of a gray/colour scale as the app background via a mutable `AppBg` alias — [Radix, Understanding the scale](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)
- Radix gray pairing: gray (neutral, works with any hue), mauve (purple), slate (blue), sage (green), olive (lime), sand (yellow); choose the gray whose hue is closest to the accent; saturated grays can clash with colourful badges especially in dark mode; semantic groupings: error = red/ruby/tomato/crimson, success = green/teal/jade/grass/mint, warning = yellow/amber/orange, info = blue/indigo/sky/cyan — [Radix, Composing a palette](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/composing-a-palette)
- A production example (Unkey) built on Radix: step-12 headings, step-11 body, step-10 tertiary/disabled, step-9 hints/uppercase labels; status callouts pair step-3 background with step-11 text; solid = step 9 with step 10 hover; accent = tuned Slate; success teal, warning amber, error tomato, info blue, "feature" violet; dark values mapped on `.dark` so components need no dark-mode variants; exposed as Tailwind utilities (`bg-gray-2`, `text-accent-12`, `border-grayA-4`) and CSS variables (`hsl(var(--accent-11))`) — [Unkey Design, Colors](https://design.unkey.com/colors)
- shadcn/ui (Tailwind v4): HSL tokens converted to OKLCH; dark-mode colours "were later revised for accessibility and moved to new OKLCH values"; `:root` and `.dark` define variables outside `@layer base` and `@theme inline` maps them to `--color-*`; chart colours referenced as `var(--chart-1)`, `var(--chart-2)` — [shadcn/ui, Tailwind v4](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/tailwind-v4)

**WCAG requirements**
- WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation on 5 October 2023; it adds 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum, AA), 2.4.12 (Enhanced, AAA), 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA: minimum size and ≥3:1 between focused/unfocused states), 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA), 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (AA: ≥24×24 CSS px or spacing exception), 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A), 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A), 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum, AA), 3.3.9 (Enhanced, AAA); 4.1.1 Parsing was removed — [W3C, What's New in WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)
- (The 4.5:1 body-text and 3:1 UI-component ratios are WCAG 2.x criteria 1.4.3 and 1.4.11 carried over unchanged; the W3C page states 2.0/2.1 criteria "otherwise remain essentially the same" — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/).)

**TED's red and visual identity**
- TED's official TEDx logo guidance: "TED Red: HEX #EB0028 (Pantone 185)"; "TEDx" must be red and the "x" cannot be recoloured; the place name must be black or white; backgrounds must be solid white or black only; typeface Helvetica (or Inter Light if unavailable; Helvetica Neue Regular if that version); "Don't use a typeface other than Helvetica or Inter Light" — [TED, Your TEDx Logo](https://www.ted.com/pages/your-tedx-logo)
- Third-party logo reference gives the TED red as "Pantone 485, HEX #e62b1e" with the wordmark in bold all-caps Neue Haas Grotesk/Helvetica — [Logos-World, TED logo](https://logos-world.net/ted-logo/). This conflicts with TED's own #EB0028; the TED page is the primary source. #E62B1E is the value the brief asked to document and is the one most reproduced on colour-reference sites.
- TED's identity is anchored in Helvetica's neutrality and has been consistent since the first conferences — [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/resources/case-studies/neue-helvetica-comes-into-its-own-for-ted)
- Computed (WCAG formula): white text on #EB0028 = 4.60:1; white on #E62B1E = 4.44:1 (fails AA for normal text); both reds as text on white give the same ratios. (Computed by me from the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula; not from a published source.)

**AI accent colour**
- "Inherit your existing color tokens. Don't create a parallel 'AI palette' just because AI features seem to need one"; add tokens only where the AI use case requires (confidence states are the justified example) and document the rationale; "AI-specific colors appearing nowhere else in the brand" is an early sign of a forked design system; Otter.ai softens low-certainty words with lighter gray — [Lazarev Agency, AI UX patterns for design leads](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/ai-ux-patterns-for-design-leads)

### Inferences
- Token architecture (works with shadcn/Tailwind v4 and Radix): define 12-step neutral + accent + status scales per theme, then alias semantic tokens: `--bg`, `--bg-subtle`, `--surface`, `--surface-raised`, `--border-subtle`, `--border`, `--border-strong` (inputs; ≥3:1 vs bg), `--text`, `--text-secondary`, `--text-muted` (≥4.5:1), `--accent`, `--accent-hover`, `--accent-text` (≥4.5:1 on bg), `--on-accent`, `--success/-warning/-danger-{bg,text,solid}`, `--ai-{bg,text,border}`, `--focus` (≥3:1 vs adjacent). Map to shadcn names (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted`, `--border`, `--input`, `--ring`, `--primary`, `--destructive`, `--chart-1…5`).

## Key Question 3: Layout and navigation for a 4-pillar, multi-step journey app — rail vs tabs vs journey map, progress visualisation, next-best-action, empty states, onboarding

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence favours a single guided sequence (Duolingo's path) with a persistent "you are here/next step" affordance and a daily "Today" surface that decomposes the long goal into one small action (Headspace); deep-linking users straight to the one recommended item and action-first onboarding ("do the thing before signing up") have measured activation gains. No fetched source directly compared left-rail vs top-tab navigation for multi-month programs, so that choice is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo replaced its skill "tree" with a single linear "path" (launched to all learners 1 November 2022) because learners "often wonder whether they're using Duolingo 'correctly' or in the 'best' way" and the company "could do better at guiding learners through lessons"; each circle is one level, completed levels turn gold and remain tappable, progress is shown "by moving down the path", circles carry descriptive topic labels with a tap-to-preview popup, and "a floating arrow returns learners to their current spot"; lesson order is built on spaced repetition and interleaving; existing progress was preserved; the post reports no outcome metrics — [Duolingo blog, New home screen design](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)
- Duolingo's Q1 2022 shareholder letter: the path "is being tested on a subset of new users with 'encouraging results'" (no metrics); DAUs 12.5M (+31% YoY), MAUs 49.2M, 2.9M paid subscribers (6.8% of MAUs); 63% of DAUs had a streak of 7+ days — [Duolingo Q1 2022 Shareholder Letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/d667f9d1-8f7f-418c-b116-e699a30a9826)
- The path redesign drew user backlash on launch (titles: "Down the wrong path: the disaster of the latest Duolingo UI update"; "Duolingo app update with path UI faces backlash") — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/down-the-wrong-path-the-disaster-of-the-latest-duolingo-ui-update-a4cdd1e6ea1c); [PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2022/11/22/duolingo-app-update-with-path-ui-faces-backlash-from-users/) (titles from search results; articles not fetched)
- Headspace's "Today" tab performs goal decomposition: "breaks large goals, such as a meditation habit, into smaller daily actions"; Peloton uses weekly rather than daily streaks; Duolingo lets users "start a lesson before signing up", which the source says raised DAU by 20%; Slack's action-based onboarding prompts users to create/join a workspace immediately instead of a carousel — [Kristen Berman, Product lessons from 6 top consumer apps](https://kristenberman.substack.com/p/product-lessons-from-6-top-consumer)
- Headspace raised engagement 34% by reducing choice overload and helping new users navigate content (details paywalled) — [InsideBE case study](https://insidebe.com/articles/how-headspace-reduced-stress-and-choice-overload)
- Headspace lifecycle program (Phiture/Braze): pushes that deep-linked directly to a specific meditation "chosen from aggregated engagement data" drove +14% first-meditation completion; week-1 retention +109% vs control for campaign recipients; paid conversions +49% within 1 day; "in-app messaging is a critical lever"; "deep-linking users to specific content is highly effective for activation" — [Phiture, Headspace retention](https://phiture.com/success-stories/headspace-retention/)
- Vercel layout grid: 12 columns desktop, 6 tablet, 4 mobile; spacing tokens 1–16 with guidance "within-group gaps use space-2 to space-4, between groups space-6 to space-8, section turns space-8 to space-12, space-16 only for true chapter breaks" — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)
- Fresh-start effect: goal-directed behaviour spikes at temporal landmarks — gym visits +33.4% at the start of a week, +14.4% start of month, +47.1% start of semester; commitment contracts +62.9% at week start and +145.3% at year start (details in Key Question 6) — [Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)

### Inferences
- Information architecture: a **persistent left rail on desktop** holding the four pillars as top-level sections (each with a completion ring), a **"Today/Next up" home** rather than a pillar list, and a **journey map** view (Duolingo-style single path with milestone nodes) reachable from the home card. On mobile collapse the rail into a bottom tab bar (Today · Journey · Record · Library · Profile). Rationale: pillars are parallel but the program is sequential; the rail gives orientation, the path gives sequence, and Today gives the one action.
- Progress visualisation: combine (a) a path/stepper with gold completed nodes and a "you are here" marker, (b) a per-pillar ring (percent complete), (c) a weekly target (e.g., "2 sessions this week"), and (d) milestone badges tied to real artefacts (first draft, first recording, first run-through). Avoid a single global percent-complete as the hero because months-long programs stay low for a long time.
- Next-best-action card: one primary CTA deep-linked to the exact step (not a list), with time estimate ("12 min"), rationale ("because you finished your outline"), and a secondary "or do this instead" option to preserve autonomy.
- Empty states should show the path with the first node unlocked and a 2-minute first action (the "aha" moment: e.g., record a 30-second idea pitch and see one AI note) — mirroring Duolingo's lesson-before-signup and Slack's action-first onboarding.
- Progressive profiling: collect only the fields needed to personalise the first week (goal talk date, topic, experience level) and request the rest contextually, consistent with WCAG 3.3.7 Redundant Entry.
- Schedule "re-start" prompts at Mondays, month starts and after holidays (fresh-start effect) rather than fixed cadence only.

### Gaps
- No fetched source directly compares left-rail vs top-tabs vs journey-map home screens with outcome data; the IA above is inferred.
- Reforge, Maven, Teachable, Kajabi, Speechify, Yoodli (navigation), Grammarly and Linear onboarding were not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Duolingo has not published outcome metrics for the path; the only indicator is "encouraging results" in the shareholder letter and documented user backlash.
- Headspace's specific choice-overload interventions sit behind InsideBE's paywall.

## Key Question 4: AI UX patterns — presenting AI drafts so users trust and edit them

### Takeaway
Converging guidance (Microsoft HAX G7–G11/G15–G17, Google PAIR, Shape of AI, practitioner pattern libraries) says: label AI output next to the content, keep it editable in place, show diffs with accept/reject at the same fidelity as the output, allow regenerate-with-instructions while keeping the previous version, explain "why" with partial explanations tied to user actions, show confidence only when it changes a decision (categorical or N-best, not fake percentages), stream with an interrupt control, and put a human approval gate before irreversible actions.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction (CHI 2019): Initially G1 "Make clear what the system can do", G2 "Make clear how well the system can do what it can do"; During interaction G3 "Time services based on context", G4 "Show contextually relevant information", G5 "Match relevant social norms", G6 "Mitigate social biases"; When wrong G7 "Support efficient invocation", G8 "Support efficient dismissal", G9 "Support efficient correction", G10 "Scope services when in doubt", G11 "Make clear why the system did what it did"; Over time G12 "Remember recent interactions", G13 "Learn from user behavior", G14 "Update and adapt cautiously", G15 "Encourage granular feedback", G16 "Convey the consequences of user actions", G17 "Provide global controls", G18 "Notify users about changes" — [Amershi et al., Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction (camera-ready PDF)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf); validated with 49 design practitioners against 20 AI products — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/); HAX toolkit applies them in four phases: initial interaction, during interaction, when the AI is wrong, and over time — [HAX Toolkit](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)
- Google PAIR (Explainability + Trust): aim for calibrated trust, not blind trust; admitting a prediction could be wrong "reduces over-reliance and disappointment over time"; use partial explanations (general-system vs specific-output), explain data use by scope/reach/removal; techniques include highlighting influential data sources, counterfactuals, example-based explanations and "what if" testing; tie explanations to user actions; show confidence only if it changes decisions — categorical (High/Medium/Low with defined cut-offs and implied actions), N-best alternatives (good for low confidence), numeric percentages need context, visualisations may only suit experts; in high-stakes cases explain why, not just what — [PAIR Guidebook, Explainability + Trust (PDF)](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/People%20+%20AI%20Guidebook%20-%20Explainability%20+%20Trust.pdf)
- Shape of AI "Trust builders" (Emily Campbell, © 2025, CC-BY-NC-SA): Caveat, Consent, Data ownership, Disclosure ("clearly label content and interactions that AI guides or produces"), Footprints ("let users trace the AI's steps from prompt to result"), Incognito Mode, Watermark — [Shape of AI, Trust builders](https://www.shapeof.ai/pattern-types/trust-builders)
- Patterns for AI-generated content: source attribution label placed "close to the content it describes, not in a footer or settings page", graduated labels (AI-generated / AI-assisted / human-authored); confidence indicators; inline editing where the output appears; regeneration with one-click retry, per-section regeneration and guiding instructions ("Make it shorter") while "the previous version stays accessible for comparison or reverting"; selective acceptance per item; track-changes diff with approve/reject; progressive disclosure; side-by-side comparison; AI content in context; fact-check prompts; a review reminder before publishing (not a block); low-friction feedback (thumbs-down, "This isn't right") — [Simon Whatley, Design patterns for AI-generated content](https://www.simonwhatley.co.uk/writing/design-patterns-for-ai-generated-content/)
- "Trust stack" (7 patterns): citations (Perplexity, Google AI Overviews), collapsible progress steps during waits, streaming with the ability to interrupt ("the interrupt control matters more than any pacing polish"), confidence bands (avoid fake precision such as percentages), human-in-the-loop before irreversible actions (auto-approve routine ones, "too many gates feel broken"), smart diff with accept/reject and short reasons, editable memory list (ChatGPT) — [AI UX Playground, Trust Stack](https://www.aiuxplayground.com/guides/trust-stack)
- Component-level guidance: ghost-text inline suggestions in muted gray with Tab to accept / Esc to dismiss (Copilot); red/green diff with accept-all, accept-by-line, reject (Cursor) — "Override has to be visible at the same fidelity as the AI output itself"; regenerate alone is insufficient; "No interaction should go more than 800ms without a visible progress signal" distinguishing "thinking" from "writing"; every error state needs retry or manual override within one click; name gaps directly (NotebookLM: "I cannot find that information in the documents you provided"); soften uncertain output typographically (Otter.ai); label the model version — [Lazarev Agency](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/ai-ux-patterns-for-design-leads)
- Trust patterns with product examples: citations (Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT inline), action plans with accept/change before an agent acts (Claude browser extension; Claude and ChatGPT list planned steps and ask for confirmation), AI-content markers (Google SynthID, Steam, Instagram); principle "trust but verify" — [Telerik, AI UX patterns for user trust](https://www.telerik.com/blogs/ai-ux-patterns-user-trust)

### Inferences
- For the talk-script editor: AI drafts appear as an "AI draft" labelled block inside the editor (not a side chat), with (1) streamed rendering plus a Stop control, (2) a "Why this" popover tied to the user's inputs (talk goal, audience, prior feedback) per PAIR/G11, (3) "Regenerate with a steer" (chips: shorter / more personal / stronger opening) that creates a new version while preserving the prior one in version history, (4) a diff view with accept-all / accept-by-paragraph / reject when AI edits existing text, (5) per-suggestion thumbs/"not right" feedback (G15), and (6) a global "AI assistance" switch and per-feature toggles (G17).
- Confidence: use categorical cues only where they change behaviour (e.g., "Needs a source" flag on claims; "Low-confidence transcript segment" shading) rather than scores.
- Publishing/sending gates: a review reminder before exporting a script or sharing a recording, with no blocking.
- Keep AI chrome inside existing tokens (one `--ai-*` set); avoid "sparkle everywhere".

### Gaps
- No NN/g articles on AI output UX could be fetched (search results did not surface NN/g pages before the budget ran out); the NN/g 2024–2026 guidance referenced in the brief is therefore uncited here.
- No controlled studies quantifying the effect of diff views, version history or "why this" explanations on edit rates or trust were found; the sources are practitioner guidance plus the HAX validation study.

## Key Question 5: Video and audio UX — recording interfaces, review screens, feedback presentation, teleprompter design, caption accessibility

### Takeaway
Recording UIs converge on a short (3-second) countdown, a mic/camera test recording, speaker notes visible only to the presenter, pause/resume and in-recording rewind (Loom); speech-coaching tools (Yoodli) report filler words, pacing, eye contact, word choice and body language on a post-recording dashboard; captions should follow DCMP's rules (white medium-weight sans with shadow or translucent box, mixed case, ≤2 lines, 1–6 s duration, ≤130–160 wpm). Education research on grades vs comments argues for leading with narrative comments and delaying or de-emphasising numeric scores.

### Cited Findings

**Recording interface**
- A recording countdown "typically lasts for three seconds" and exists so users can "start speaking or demonstrating at the moment you intend, avoiding awkward pauses at the start"; the page is a vendor glossary (Tella) and is internally inconsistent about Loom's countdown — [Tella, Recording Countdown](https://www.tella.com/definition/recording-countdown)
- Loom's recording guidance: face a window / use "adjust for low lighting"; keep the mic close, record in a quiet room, "suppress background noise"; "Do a quick test recording to confirm your mic picks up your voice clearly and your video looks good"; clean background or virtual backgrounds; speaker notes "visible only to you while recording"; Live Rewind to trim while recording; Pause and Resume; drawing tool; stable connection — [Loom (Atlassian) Recording tips](https://support.atlassian.com/loom/docs/recording-tips-for-a-great-video)
- Teleprompter placement: text as close to the lens as possible; window directly above/below/beside the webcam, within inches on external monitors; adjust monitor height to look slightly up or straight ahead; mount a phone/tablet directly behind the camera — [Speakflow](https://www.speakflow.com/blog/posts/free-online-teleprompter-guide)

**Review/feedback (speech coaching)**
- Yoodli's post-recording dashboard reports filler words (e.g., "um", "ah"), pacing, eye contact, word choice and body language; practice modes are Presentation (upload slides or impromptu), Interview (AI-generated questions) and Roleplay (e.g., salary negotiation) — [PolyU ELC, Exploring CILL AI Tools: Yoodli (PDF)](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/elc/docdrive/genai_at_elc/GenAI@ELC_Exploring_CILL_AI_Tools-Yoodli.pdf)
- Yoodli overview: Roleplays with AI personas, AI Tutor (walks through material, quizzes), AI Coach that "tracks your progress over time and provides coaching before, during, and after practice", multi-persona roleplay, works with Zoom/Teams/Meet, transcripts and feedback follow the selected practice language and regional English variant — [Yoodli support, Yoodli Overview](https://support.yoodli.ai/en/articles/9550461-yoodli-overview)
- Toastmasters has an official partnership with Yoodli ("AI Meets Public Speaking") — [Toastmasters](https://www.toastmasters.org/about/partnerships/yoodli) (title from search results; page not fetched)

**Scores vs narrative**
- Butler's 1988 study (as summarised): grades-only left students complacent or unmotivated; grades-with-comments were "equally poorly served" because students "jumped straight to the number"; comments-only showed the most improvement — [Spaces EDU, Focusing on feedback over grades](https://spacesedu.com/en/focusing-on-feedback-over-grades/) (secondary summary; original study not fetched)
- A 2017 quasi-experiment (n=60) found adding delayed scores after comments did not hurt performance, students said scores mattered less on ungraded tasks, some said early scores could cause complacency or discouragement; it cites Butler 1988 and Lipnevich & Smith 2009 for grades weakening the effect of comments and notes contrary evidence (Smith & Gorard 2005); recommendations: descriptive comments that pinpoint mistakes and ask critical-thinking questions, quick return with time for self-correction, "withhold suggested answers and release scores only after self-correction" — [Chua, Lee & Fulmer 2017, Asia-Pacific Science Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41029-017-0015-y)
- PAIR: avoid numeric confidence without context; prefer categorical levels with implied actions or N-best alternatives — [PAIR Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/People%20+%20AI%20Guidebook%20-%20Explainability%20+%20Trust.pdf)

**Teleprompter**
- 125–150 wpm typical; scroll 5–10% slower than feels right; 1–2 sentences per line; highlight line guide; (pause) cues — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/script-speed-tips-for-teleprompter-delivery); large font for peripheral reading, narrow centred column, high contrast, mirror mode for beam-splitter rigs, keyboard speed shortcuts — [Speakflow](https://www.speakflow.com/blog/posts/free-online-teleprompter-guide)

**Captions**
- DCMP Captioning Key: white, medium-weight, proportionally spaced sans-serif with drop/rim shadow, translucent box preferred; bottom two lines, left-aligned when possible, moved to the top when covering faces/mouths/graphics; no more than two lines preferred; minimum duration 40 frames (1 s 10 f), maximum 6 s; presentation rate ≤130 wpm (lower-level), ≤140 (middle), ≤160 (upper), verbatim for quoted speakers; mixed case ("all capitals are reserved for screaming or shouting"); synchronised to audio; speaker ID by placement or "(name)" on its own line; sound effects in brackets only when essential — [DCMP Captioning Key](https://dcmp.org/captioningkey/print)
- Vercel: provide text alternatives for charts and keep the experience complete without motion — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)

### Inferences
- Recording flow: (1) pre-flight panel with live camera preview, a framing guide (head-and-shoulders silhouette and eye-line rule-of-thirds line), input-level meter with a 5-second "say your name" mic test and a playback button, lighting warning; (2) a 3-second countdown (skippable in settings); (3) during recording, a timer, teleprompter/notes overlay positioned just below the camera, pause/resume, "restart take"; (4) post-take, trim and "keep/retake".
- Review screen: video left, interactive transcript right synced word-by-word (click to seek), a metrics timeline under the scrubber with markers for filler words, pace spikes (>170 wpm) and long pauses, and highlight markers the user and coach can add. Lead with 2–3 narrative "what worked / try next" notes; show numbers (filler count, wpm, eye-contact %) second, framed against the user's own previous take rather than a universal score, per the grades-vs-comments research. Offer a "hide scores" preference.
- Avoid a single composite "speech score" as the headline; if a score exists, present it as a trend sparkline across takes.
- Captions: default on for all playback, DCMP-style rendering (white #FFFFFF on 75% black ≈ 10:1 computed), user-adjustable size, and keyboard-accessible transcript.

## Key Question 6: Motivation and habit design for a months-long goal — streaks, weekly plans, nudges, cadence, celebration, drop-off evidence

### Takeaway
Measured mechanics with published effects: commitment devices (Duolingo streak wager: +14% day-7 retention), streak protection (weekend amulet: +4% weekly return, 5% fewer streak losses), lifecycle messaging deep-linked to one action (Headspace: +109% week-1 retention vs control), and timing nudges to temporal landmarks (fresh-start effect: +33% gym visits at week start, +145% commitment contracts at year start). Weekly rather than daily streaks (Peloton) and flexible/forgiving streaks mitigate streak anxiety and post-break abandonment.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo streak wager A/B: learners could spend in-game currency to commit to 7 more streak days and win double back; day-1/7/14 retention rose significantly, day-7 by +14%; Weekend Amulet (preserve streak over the weekend, targeting a 5–10% weekend DAU dip): +4% likelihood of returning a week later, 5% less likely to lose the streak; the team argues giving learners "an option to take breaks" leads to more long-term engagement and that binge learners are more likely to quit than pacers; principles: "marathon rather than a sprint", data/A-B driven — [Duolingo blog, How streaks keep learners committed](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/)
- 63% of Duolingo DAUs had a 7+ day streak at end of Q1 2022, up 16% YoY; social sharing up 140% since start of 2022; Kudos between friends — [Duolingo Q1 2022 Shareholder Letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/d667f9d1-8f7f-418c-b116-e699a30a9826)
- Duolingo ran "hundreds of experiments in 2022" including mood-changing widgets when a lesson is skipped, streak-milestone app icons, goal-commitment copy and Perfect Streak Week — but the aggregator has no quantified results and relies on a single social-media thread — [Lazyweb (aggregator; treat as pointer only)](https://lazyweb.com/research/duolingo-retention-experiments)
- Fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis, Management Science 2014, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901): Google "diet" searches +14.4% at week start, +3.7% month start, +82.1% year start, +10.2% after federal holidays; gym visits (11,912 students) +33.4% week start, +14.4% month start, +11.6% year start, +47.1% semester start, +24.3% after breaks, +7.5% after birthdays; stickK commitment contracts (66,062) +62.9% week start, +23.6% month start, +145.3% year start, +55.1% after holidays; mechanism: landmarks open new "mental accounting periods" and prompt big-picture thinking; effects fade as the landmark recedes — [Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 (PDF)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)
- Headspace CRM program: week-1 retention +109% vs control, +49% paid conversion within a day, +14% first-meditation completion from rich pushes deep-linked to a data-selected meditation; iterative rounds kept adding gains (6% then a further 20% activation) — [Phiture](https://phiture.com/success-stories/headspace-retention/)
- Peloton's streak is weekly ("one workout a week was enough"), making momentum easier to maintain; a long-time member churned partly because plans had "no sense of progression or adjustment over time"; RevenueCat's takeaways include "help users get value fast", "explain how recommendations are personalized", "reward loyal customers rather than penalize them", and simple cancellation — [RevenueCat, Peloton retention takeaways](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/peloton-retention-takeaways)
- Welcome-back offers (Duolingo 300 gems), pausable streaks, reminders timed to availability, Strava's small-consistent-improvement framing and friend-activity notifications, Headspace "Today" goal decomposition — [Kristen Berman](https://kristenberman.substack.com/p/product-lessons-from-6-top-consumer)
- Arguments against streaks: stakes rise with length ("365 days? Devastating"), a broken long streak "could be enough to put people off attempting to get back on track", minimum-effort behaviour to protect streaks (rushed lesson before midnight, skipping to the end of a Headspace session), shift from intrinsic to extrinsic motivation, sunk-cost pressure, streaks assume "an idealised version of a user"; alternatives: weekly or monthly counts, keep the base goal achievable with daily completion as a bonus, combine with XP/milestones, forgiveness on reset; evidence is anecdotal, no studies cited — [UX Collective, Gamification gone wrong: stop the streaks](https://uxdesign.cc/gamification-gone-wrong-stop-the-streaks-c3de42618ae)
- Feedback-order research (comments before scores, self-correction time) — [Chua et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41029-017-0015-y)

### Inferences
- Use a **weekly commitment** as the core unit (e.g., "3 practice blocks this week"), shown as a 7-dot row, with an optional daily "momentum" count that can be paused or frozen; celebrate weeks, not days.
- Offer an explicit **commitment device** at the start of each pillar (choose a target date and weekly load; optional accountability partner/coach), modelled on the streak-wager effect without the monetary stake.
- **Nudge cadence:** one scheduled weekly plan email (Sunday evening/Monday morning, aligned to the fresh-start effect), one mid-week in-app/push nudge deep-linked to the single next step, and milestone-triggered messages; SMS only for time-critical items (recording slots, coach sessions). Re-engagement campaigns timed to month starts and after holidays.
- **Celebration moments** tied to artefacts (first full draft, first 5-minute recording, first run-through under time) rather than abstract points; share-ready cards for LinkedIn given Duolingo's social-sharing growth.
- **Drop-off recovery:** when a user misses a week, show a "fresh start" screen that resets the weekly target without shaming, offers a smaller next step, and preserves accumulated progress (per Duolingo's break-taking principle and the streak-criticism piece).

### Gaps
- No published drop-off curves for coaching/learning programs of 3–6 months (Reforge, Maven, Kajabi, Teachable) were found; Noom was not researched (search budget).
- Duolingo's modern streak freeze/repair/Streak Society effects are not in the fetched primary sources (the 2017 post predates them; the 2022 letter does not mention them).
- Goal-gradient research (Kivetz et al.) was not fetched; only the fresh-start paper is cited.
- No evidence on SMS vs email cadence for professional-audience coaching products was found.

## Key Question 7: Accessibility and internationalisation — WCAG 2.2 AA essentials, editor keyboard access, reduced motion, RTL/Indian languages, timezones

### Takeaway
For this product the most relevant WCAG 2.2 additions are Target Size (24×24 px), Dragging Movements (give non-drag alternatives for timeline scrubbing and reorder), Focus Not Obscured (sticky toolbars must not hide focus), Redundant Entry (progressive profiling must not re-ask), Consistent Help, and Accessible Authentication; combine with 4.5:1/3:1 contrast, 200% zoom, ≤80 cpl, captions per DCMP, and reduced-motion fallbacks. Indic support is practical via Noto Sans Devanagari (variable, OFL, 78 languages).

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 criteria and levels (see Key Question 2): 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Min, AA); 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA) — "any function that uses dragging must also work with a single pointer press"; 2.5.8 Target Size (Min, AA) — "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels" or spacing exception; 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A); 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A) — information already given must be auto-filled or selectable; 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Min, AA) — no memorisation/puzzle tests without alternatives — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)
- Reading/zoom: test at 200% zoom (WCAG 1.4.4), max 80 cpl (1.4.8), use relative units, left-align, provide user controls for size/spacing/background — [UXPin](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/optimal-line-length-for-readability/)
- Motion and non-colour cues: "Respect reduced-motion preferences, and keep the base experience complete without motion"; "Never rely on color alone"; visible focus; native controls; semantic tables; text alternatives for charts — [vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)
- Captions: DCMP rules (Key Question 5) — [DCMP](https://dcmp.org/captioningkey/print)
- Indic typography: Noto Sans Devanagari (Google/Noto Project, OFL-1.1) covers 78 languages including Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit, Maithili, Bhojpuri, Konkani, Dogri, Sindhi; 954 glyphs; nine weights in one variable file (wght 100–900, wdth 62.5–100) — [Fontsource, Noto Sans Devanagari](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-devanagari/about)
- Helvetica's "broad language support was a key factor" for TED "given the many languages TED works with" — [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/resources/case-studies/neue-helvetica-comes-into-its-own-for-ted)
- Yoodli adapts transcripts and feedback to the selected practice language and English regional variant — [Yoodli](https://support.yoodli.ai/en/articles/9550461-yoodli-overview)
- Editor accessibility: Lexical (Meta, MIT) is noted for "accessibility work" and official React bindings — [Velt, Best rich text editors 2026](https://velt.dev/blog/best-rich-text-editors-react-2026)

### Inferences
- Timeline scrubbers, highlight markers and path re-ordering must have click/keyboard equivalents (2.5.7): arrow-key seeking, "add marker here" buttons, up/down move controls.
- All icon buttons, transcript word targets and path nodes ≥24×24 px with ≥8 px spacing (2.5.8); transcript word-level seeking should use whole-word hit areas or line-level targets.
- Sticky editor toolbars and AI side panels must not cover the focused element (2.4.11); keep a bottom safe area.
- Editor keyboard model: standard shortcuts, Tab accept / Esc dismiss for AI ghost text, a documented shortcut sheet, and a roving tabindex for toolbars; use `prefers-reduced-motion` to disable streaming "typewriter" animation (render in chunks instead).
- Font stack: `Inter/Geist, "Noto Sans Devanagari", "Noto Sans", system-ui` with `unicode-range` subsetting; verify line-height ≥1.5 for Devanagari ascenders/descenders; plan for Tamil/Bengali/Gurmukhi via the corresponding Noto families.
- RTL: use CSS logical properties (`margin-inline-start`), `dir="auto"` on user-generated text fields and transcripts, mirrored progress paths; teleprompter and caption alignment must follow text direction.
- Timezones: store deadlines as UTC instants plus the user's IANA zone; display "due Fri 6 pm (IST)"; schedule weekly nudges in local time; show countdowns relative to the talk date with the zone named.

### Gaps
- No source on RTL-specific UI patterns or on Indian-language UX (e.g., Hinglish input, transliteration) was fetched; search budget exhausted before these queries.
- No fetched source covers timezone handling for deadline-driven learning products; the guidance above is standard engineering practice, uncited.
- WCAG 2.2 text-spacing (1.4.12) and non-text contrast (1.4.11) were not fetched directly; only referenced via the W3C statement that earlier criteria carry over.

## Key Question 8: Component libraries and tooling for a Next.js/React build — shadcn/ui, Radix, Tailwind v4, editors, motion, charts, video players

### Takeaway
shadcn/ui on Tailwind v4 + React 19 (OKLCH tokens, `data-slot`, `new-york` default, Sonner toasts, `tw-animate-css`) plus Radix Colors is the mainstream 2026 stack; for the script editor Tiptap (ProseMirror-based, ~1.8M monthly npm downloads, headless, Yjs collaboration) or Lexical (Meta, MIT) are the mature choices and Slate/Plate remain beta; for video, Cloudflare Stream is cheapest ($5/1,000 min stored, $1/1,000 min delivered, free encoding/ingress) while Mux offers per-second billing, a $20 monthly credit and deeper QoE analytics.

### Cited Findings
- shadcn/ui Tailwind v4: CLI initialises Tailwind v4 projects; all components updated for React 19 and Tailwind v4 while v3/React 18 apps keep working; `@theme inline` support; HSL → OKLCH; dark-mode colours "revised for accessibility"; `forwardRef` removed (props typed as `React.ComponentProps<...>`); every primitive gets `data-slot`; `new-york` is the default style (`default` deprecated); `toast` deprecated in favour of `sonner`; `tailwindcss-animate` deprecated in favour of `tw-animate-css`; `size-*` utilities; chart config references `var(--chart-1)` — [shadcn/ui, Tailwind v4](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/tailwind-v4)
- Radix Colors scales, APCA guarantees and gray pairing — [Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale); production use with Tailwind utilities — [Unkey](https://design.unkey.com/colors)
- Editors (2026): Tiptap — open source with commercial options, "over 1.8 million monthly npm downloads", headless, extension system, TypeScript, Yjs multiplayer (infrastructure self-configured), premium plugins; built on ProseMirror; Lexical — MIT, Meta (Facebook/WhatsApp), minimal core + plugins, official React bindings, performance and accessibility focus, needs more setup, collaboration via third party; Slate — MIT, "still in beta" with breaking API changes; Plate — 30+ plugins on top of Slate, inherits beta status; BlockNote — Notion-style blocks, slash commands, moderate bundle; Quill — BSD, Slab, ~100 KB; CKEditor 5 — open-source core with commercial collaboration/track changes/revision history; TinyMCE — commercial premium plugins — [Velt, Best rich text editors 2026](https://velt.dev/blog/best-rich-text-editors-react-2026)

## Key Question 9: Brand naming and logo guidance — avoiding implied TED affiliation

### Takeaway
TED's rules are explicit even for its own licensees: never use the TED logo, never present TEDx as a co-brand or as another entity's offering, name only as "TEDx + location", and state independence ("This independent TEDx event is operated under license from TED"; "x = independently organized"). A non-affiliated product should therefore keep TED/TEDx out of its name and logo, use "TED-style"/"TEDx-style" only descriptively in body copy, carry a clear non-affiliation disclaimer, and avoid the red + black/white + Helvetica/Inter-Light look that TED prescribes.

### Cited Findings
- "You should never use the TED logo in any communications or branding"; events are named "TEDx" + location with "TED" capitalised and lowercase "x"; always use the full licensed name, no abbreviations; "Never refer to the event simply as 'TEDx'"; TEDx events "cannot be co-branded with other organizations, and they cannot be presented as offerings of another entity" and "must not be combined with or integrated into other conferences, ceremonies, or curricula"; the footer "must say 'This independent TEDx event is operated under license from TED'"; organizers must state the event is "independently organized, and not a TED event"; "The 'x' in 'TEDx' stands for 'independently organized events'"; speakers "cannot promote their own products, books, or businesses"; organizers may not create podcasts based on TEDx content; anyone may use up to 30 seconds of a talk; TEDx-branded products must carry the unique event name, not the generic TEDx logo; TED and TEDx logos may not appear on content without the location qualifier — [TED, TEDx Rules](https://www.ted.com/pages/tedx_rules)
- TEDx logo rules: Helvetica (fallback Inter Light), TED Red #EB0028, place name in black or white, solid black or white backgrounds only, no colour/photo backgrounds; "Don't lock the logo up with other logos or images" — [TED, Your TEDx Logo](https://www.ted.com/pages/your-tedx-logo)
- TED's visual identity is Helvetica-based and has been consistent since its first conferences — [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/resources/case-studies/neue-helvetica-comes-into-its-own-for-ted); a third-party reference describes the wordmark as bold all-caps Helvetica/Neue Haas Grotesk in red (#E62B1E) on white — [Logos-World](https://logos-world.net/ted-logo/)

### Inferences
- Naming: do not use "TED", "TEDx", "TEDx-ready", "TEDstyle" or an uppercase three-letter red wordmark in the product name, domain, app icon or logo. Use a distinct name that describes the outcome (stage, talk, idea, keynote, spotlight) and refer to TEDx only in descriptive copy: "prepare a TEDx-style talk", "for speakers applying to independently organized TEDx events".
- Disclaimer (footer, app store listing, onboarding, marketing): "[Product] is an independent product and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by TED Conferences, LLC. TED and TEDx are trademarks of TED Conferences, LLC." (Wording modelled on TED's own independence language; have counsel confirm the entity name and the nominative-fair-use scope.)
- Visual distance: avoid a vivid red primary, avoid black/white-only backgrounds with a red wordmark, and avoid Helvetica/Neue Helvetica/Inter Light as the logo face; prefer the Ink & Ivory palette with a gold accent and a serif-led wordmark (Fraunces/Instrument Serif) so that even a glance does not read as TED.
- Content: never embed TED/TEDx talk video beyond the 30-second excerpt allowance without the licence terms being checked; link out to ted.com instead.

### Gaps
- No trademark-law source (likelihood-of-confusion doctrine, nominative fair use cases) was fetched; the search budget was exhausted before legal sources could be checked. The recommendations are design/brand hygiene, not legal advice.
- TED's general (non-TEDx) trademark policy page for third parties was not located; the fetched TEDx Rules page states "The page has no separate section on what third parties may or may not do".
- Examples of how comparable products (speaker-coaching apps) word their TED disclaimers were not found.
