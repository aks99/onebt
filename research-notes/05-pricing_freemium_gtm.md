## Key Question 1: Freemium / free-trial benchmarks (conversion, time-to-convert, free-tier design, churn for prosumer SaaS at $100+/mo)

### Takeaway
Across the two most current B2B datasets (ChartMogul Feb 2026, n=200; Lenny/OpenView 2023, n=1,000+), "good" freemium free-to-paid conversion is roughly 3–5% and "great" is 8–12% within 6 months, while no-credit-card free trials run 4–6% good / 10–15% great and credit-card-required trials 25–35% good / 50–60% great; reverse trials land at 4–6% / 8–12% on a small sample. Churn falls sharply with price: ARPA under $25/mo churns ~6.1%/month vs ~2.2%/month above $500/mo (ChartMogul, 2021–22 data), and AI-native products above $250/mo retain ~70% of revenue vs ~23% below $50/mo (CRV, 2025), which supports pricing a serious prosumer product above $100/mo rather than below.

**Time-to-convert and reverse trials**
- Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged): free-trial products often see 2–3x the free-to-paid conversion of freemium; median time-to-purchase is ~14 days for free-trial companies vs 60–90 days for freemium. Airtable runs a 14-day reverse trial of its Pro plan that falls back to Free; rule of thumb for gating is ~80% of users stay free, ~20% upgrade; a 2–3% free-to-paid baseline reaching 5% within 3 months is described as a major improvement; loss aversion (losing ≈2x as motivating as gaining) is the rationale for reverse trials. No reverse-trial conversion figure is given. — [Growth Unhinged: Your guide to reverse trials](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/your-guide-to-reverse-trials)
- ChartMogul 2026 reverse-trial benchmark: 4–6% good / 8–12% great (small sample). — [ChartMogul Conversion Report 2026](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)

**Consumer/prosumer app benchmarks (RevenueCat, 2026)**
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000+ apps, >$16B revenue): hard-paywall Day-35 trial-to-paid median 10.7% (12.1% in 2025); freemium Day-35 trial-to-paid 2.1%; trials of 17–32 days convert at 42.5% median vs 25.5% for trials of ≤4 days; 46.5% of apps use trials of ≤4 days; yearly subscribers retained after 1 year: 28% (freemium) / 27% (hard paywall); 35% of annual cancellations occur in Month 1; ~72% of annual subscribers cancel within Year 1 in 2026 (up from ~56% in 2025); revenue per install at Day 60 is $3.09 for hard-paywall vs $0.38 for freemium (~8x); AI apps earn 41% more revenue per user in Year 1 ($30.16 vs $21.37 realized LTV) but churn 36% faster, and AI monthly plans retain 36% worse over 12 months. — [RevenueCat 2026 trends & benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)

- RevenueCat trial-length analysis (2026): median app-level trial conversion for monthly plans is 39.6% (≤4-day trial), 45.9% (5–9 days), 46.6% (10–16 days), 43.7% (17–32 days); for annual plans 24% / 33% / 43% / 44.6%. **Churn benchmarks (by price point / segment)**
- ChartMogul (data Nov 2021–Jan 2022; article Feb 2022; sample size not stated): median monthly customer churn 6.1% for ARPA under $25/mo vs 2.2% for ARPA over $500/mo; 6.5% for companies under $300k ARR vs 3.7% in the $1–3M MRR band; "best" companies run <2% monthly (<1% at higher ARPA); top-quartile retention 85.8% for ARPA >$1k/mo vs 64.7% for ARPA <$25/mo; 5% monthly churn ≈ 46% annual. The $25–100 and $100–500 ARPA bands exist only in a chart image. (Older data: 2021–22.) — [ChartMogul: What is a good customer churn rate](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/)
- ProfitWell / Patrick Campbell study of 3,000+ subscription companies (summarized by Reforge, Oct 2019 — old data): monthly gross revenue churn for ARPU under $100 is 3–16% (median 6–9%) vs ARPU over $500 2–6% (median ~3–4%); companies under 3 years old churn 4–24%/month vs 2–4% for 10+ year-old companies. — [Reforge: churn benchmarks](https://www.reforge.com/blog/brief-churn-benchmarks-for-recurring-revenue-businesses)
- CRV (2025): AI-native companies show median gross revenue retention of 40% and median NRR of 48% vs ~101% NRR for broader SaaS; products priced above $250/month retain 70% of revenue while products below $50/month retain 23% ("low-price tiers attract experimenters who churn quickly"). — [CRV: AI SaaS revenue models](https://www.crv.com/content/ai-saas-revenue-models)
- Lighter Capital 2025 B2B SaaS Startup Benchmarks (Sept 2025; 155 private B2B SaaS startups in US/Canada/Australia, CY2020–2024): median annual customer churn 16.25% (2025) vs 16.21% (2024), 25th/75th percentile 9.35%/27.25%; median annual revenue churn 12.5% (2025); in the Education vertical, customer churn roughly doubled from 11% to 22% and revenue churn rose 71% year over year. — [Lighter Capital 2025 benchmarks](https://www.lightercapital.com/blog/2025-b2b-saas-startup-benchmarks)
- Recurly churn benchmarks (data period July 2026; sample not stated): SaaS total churn 3.22% (2.16% voluntary, 1.06% involuntary); Education 4.99% (3.30% voluntary, 1.69% involuntary) — the highest of the listed categories; Business & professional services 3.44%; B2C e-commerce 4.25%. The fetched page text labels these as annual figures, but the magnitudes match Recurly's historically monthly benchmark series, so treat the period basis as ambiguous. — [Recurly churn benchmarks](https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks)
- Lenny's Newsletter retention benchmarks (2021-era post, still widely cited): Consumer SaaS 6-month user retention good ~40% / great ~70%, 12-month net revenue retention good ~55% / great ~80%; SMB/mid-market SaaS 6-month user retention ~60% / ~80%; prosumer (bottom-up) 12-month NRR good ~100% / great ~120%. — [Lenny's Newsletter: What is good retention](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29)
- An unsourced 2025 roundup claims SMB-focused SaaS runs 5–7% annual churn, "monthly billing churns 2–3x more than annual," and EdTech ~9–10% churn; it names no data sources, so treat as directional only. — [UserJot churn benchmarks](https://userjot.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- For a TEDx-guidance product sold to coaches/consultants/authors (a B2C/B2B hybrid, AI-native, education-adjacent category), a realistic planning assumption is 3–5% freemium-to-paid (good) with 8–12% as a stretch; the Education/EdTech-specific freemium figure (2.1%) and education churn data suggest planning conservatively and leaning on trial mechanics (reverse trial or no-card trial) rather than pure freemium.
- The evidence favors a reverse trial (14 days of the premium tier, then fall back to a limited free tier): it combines freemium's higher sign-up rate (~9% vs ~5%) with trial-like urgency and converts at 4–6% good / 8–12% great in ChartMogul's 2026 data; free trials also convert ~4–6x faster (14 days vs 60–90 days), which matters for a lifecycle product.
- Churn data consistently shows price is a retention lever: pricing the core paid tier at $100+ and the premium tier at $250+ should structurally reduce churn vs sub-$50 pricing; the free tier should be designed to filter out "experimenters" (usage caps on AI, not just feature gates).
- Annual plans need month-1 onboarding attention: consumer data shows 35% of annual cancellations happen in month 1, so the annual offer should be bundled with onboarding calls/concierge rather than only a discount.

### Gaps
- No source publishes free-to-paid conversion specifically for prosumer products priced $100–$500/mo; ChartMogul gives ARPA mix but not conversion by ARPA band.
- Reverse-trial benchmarks are thin (ChartMogul flags its own sample as not statistically significant; Growth Unhinged gives no number).
- No benchmark found for time-to-convert by ARPA band or for "usage cap vs feature gate" A/B results beyond Poyar's 80/20 heuristic.
- Monthly churn benchmarks specifically for $100–$500/mo prosumer SaaS in 2024–2026 were not found; the closest are ChartMogul's 2021–22 ARPA bands and CRV's 2025 AI-native retention by price.

## Key Question 2: Comparable products priced ~$100–$500/mo for solo professionals and creators (tiers, prices, gating, bundled coaching/community)

### Takeaway
The creator/prosumer category clusters around three paid tiers of roughly $80–$180 (entry), $180–$250 (mid, usually "most popular") and $350–$500 (top), with annual discounts of 10–33%, declining platform transaction fees as a tier-upgrade incentive, and AI metered by credits; human help (onboarding, concierge, expert sessions) is reserved for the top tier or annual plans, and the dedicated speaker-coaching market sells one-time programs ($37 to ~$1,500 for courses/short coaching; customized "financed" pricing for 6-month cohorts) rather than subscriptions.

## Key Question 4: AI cost pass-through (credits, fair-use caps, "AI minutes"), gross-margin targets, and video-analysis cost handling

### Takeaway
AI-heavy creator tools almost universally meter AI with monthly credit allotments that scale by tier (Descript 100 one-time free → 400/800/1,500; Opus 60 → 150/300; Circle 2,500/2,500/5,000; Kajabi 0/500/1,000; Riverside 2,000 → 12,000), sell top-ups at a premium (Circle $10/1,000; Kajabi $15/500; Clay +30%), and degrade or watermark outputs on free tiers; AI-native gross margins run ~50–70% (Jasper publicly 50–60%) vs 75–85% for classic SaaS, with practitioners advising a base price set so the median user yields ~60% margin, a fair-use cap at ~3x median usage, and 90-day repricing reviews.

## Key Question 6: Payment and pricing infrastructure for US/Canada/UK/India (Stripe vs Paddle vs Lemon Squeezy, MoR, tax, India/UPI, PPP, fees)

### Takeaway
Stripe (direct) costs 2.9% + 30¢ domestic plus 1.5% international and 1% FX, with optional Stripe Tax (0.5%/txn) and a new Stripe Managed Payments merchant-of-record add-on at +3.5%; Paddle and Lemon Squeezy both charge 5% + 50¢ as merchants of record handling sales tax/VAT; for India, Paddle supports UPI one-time and (since June 17, 2026) UPI Autopay recurring in INR with a ₹15,000 per-renewal cap, while Stripe India is invite-only and Stripe Managed Payments is not supported there; PPP pricing is widely recommended (India commonly ~50–75% below US) but no measured uplift data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe US standard pricing: 2.9% + 30¢ per domestic card; +1.5% international cards; +1% currency conversion; Billing 0.7% of volume (pay-as-you-go); Stripe Tax Basic 0.5% per transaction (or $0.50/txn via API), Tax Complete from $90/mo; ACH 0.8% capped at $5; disputes $15; Stripe Managed Payments (merchant of record) 3.5% per transaction on top of payments fees. — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)
- Stripe India pricing: 2% for India-issued Visa/Mastercard; 3% for international cards (+2% if conversion), 3.5% international Amex, 4.3% for international cards with USD/other-currency presentment (+2% conversion); Adaptive Pricing conversion fee from 2%; disputes ₹1,000; standard pricing is "request an invite." — [Stripe India pricing](https://stripe.com/in/pricing)
- Paddle: pay-as-you-go 5% + 50¢ per checkout transaction, no monthly fees; handles sales tax and VAT collection, filing and remittance as merchant of record; products under $10 need bespoke pricing; chargebacks and fraud protection included. — [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)
- Paddle UPI: available for INR-priced items to customers with Indian addresses; one-time purchases supported; subscriptions via UPI Autopay e-mandates; max ₹100,000 per transaction and ₹15,000 per subscription renewal; renewals take ≥24 hours after a pre-debit notification per NPCI rules; upgrades require a new mandate (renewal fails, subscription goes past_due). — [Paddle UPI docs](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/payment-methods/upi)

- PPP guidance (Dodo Payments, 2026): illustrative PPP factor of 0.3 for India ($100 → $30); candidate markets are those 20–50% below US willingness to pay; suggested guardrails: floor at 20% of US price, ceiling 300%; implement via geo-IP/locale, per-currency price IDs, localized checkout and local methods; mitigate VPN abuse by requiring local billing address/payment method and IP checks. No measured results. — [Dodo Payments PPP guide](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/purchasing-power-parity-pricing-saas)
- PPP illustration (Datadab): India 74% off ($50 → $13), Brazil 56% off, tiered discounts of 10–50% by PPP band; tools named: Purchasing Power Parity API, ParityBar. No case-study numbers. — [Datadab PPP for SaaS](https://www.datadab.com/blog/purchasing-power-parity-saas/)
- RevenueCat 2026: involuntary (billing-failure) cancellations are 31% of Google Play cancellations and 14% on App Store — a reminder that dunning matters at prosumer price points. — [RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Thinkific and Kajabi publish US-dollar prices only; Thinkific notes Stripe/PayPal gateway fees vary by plan and Kajabi's own payments rate improves by tier (2.9% → 2.7% + 30¢). — [Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/); [Kajabi pricing](https://kajabi.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- For a US/Canada/UK core with meaningful India demand, a merchant-of-record (Paddle) removes VAT/GST registration burden and is the only one of the three with documented UPI Autopay for INR subscriptions; the cost is ~2 points more than Stripe direct (5% + 50¢ vs ~2.9–4.4% + 30¢), which at $249/mo is ~$5/mo per customer.

## Key Question 7: Go-to-market patterns (webinar/challenge funnels, founder-led LinkedIn, partnerships, affiliate programs, CAC/LTV)

### Takeaway
The dominant speaker-education funnel is free live training → short assessment call → financed high-ticket program, with a $1–$297 low-ticket ladder and money-back guarantees; affiliate programs in the creator-platform category pay 30–40% recurring (Thinkific 30% recurring with 90-day cookie; Podia 30% capped at 12 months; GoHighLevel 40% recurring); the only webinar benchmark reached is a 47.7% registrant-to-attendee rate (Livestorm, 2026), and no prosumer-specific CAC/LTV benchmark was retrievable after the search budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
- The Speaker Lab funnel: free 45-minute live workshop/training, free 15-minute "Speaker Business Assessment," free fee calculator, $1 book-plus-templates pack, $297 one-day live bootcamp (no replay, money-back), then a customized-price 6-month program with 3–36-month financing. — [Speaker Lab store](https://thespeakerlab.com/store); [Speaker Lab bootcamp](https://thespeakerlab.com/bootcamp); [Speaker Lab program cost](https://thespeakerlab.com/?p=3198)
- Average webinar registrant-to-attendance rate: 47.7% (Livestorm, article dated Oct 21, 2026; underlying data source not described). — [Livestorm webinar statistics](https://livestorm.co/blog/webinar-statistics)
- Affiliate commissions: GoHighLevel 40% recurring monthly commissions with monthly payouts — [GoHighLevel affiliates](https://www.gohighlevel.com/affiliates); Thinkific 30% of recurring revenue for the life of the customer (Plus referrals $150/mo flat), 90-day cookie, 30-day hold, paid on the 13th, $25 minimum — [Thinkific affiliates](https://www.thinkific.com/affiliates/); Podia 30% on sales capped at 12 months, 90-day cookie, 30-day hold, $50 minimum — [Podia affiliates](https://affiliates.podia.com/); Skool and Kajabi list affiliate features but their commission pages were unreachable — [Skool pricing](https://www.skool.com/pricing).
- Sales-assist on free users: 44% of free-trial companies have sales reach out to >50% of sign-ups (vs 24% of freemium companies) (2023). — [Lenny's Newsletter free-to-paid](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)
- OpenView/Pendo 2023 Product Benchmarks (1,000+ respondents): tracking PQLs/PQAs raised the likelihood of fast growth by 61%, outreach to free sign-ups by 28%, a dedicated growth team by 17%, and TikTok promotion by 14%; 47% of PLG companies under $1M ARR already have a sales team. (2023 data.) — [OpenView 2023 Product Benchmarks](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)
- Lighter Capital 2025: median sales-and-marketing multiple (revenue per $ of S&M spend, ex-salaries) fell from 6.08 (2024) to 3.19 (2025) across 155 B2B SaaS startups; CAC payback and LTV:CAC are not reported. — [Lighter Capital 2025](https://www.lightercapital.com/blog/2025-b2b-saas-startup-benchmarks)
- CRV: usage-priced companies reach 115–125% NRR vs subscription-only peers struggling to stay above 100%. — [CRV AI SaaS revenue models](https://www.crv.com/content/ai-saas-revenue-models)
- Taplio (LinkedIn growth tool used for founder-led content) prices at $39/$69/$199 per month, with Pro adding automated lead outreach — indicating the tooling cost of a founder-led LinkedIn motion. — [Taplio pricing](https://taplio.com/pricing)
- Impact Eleven's model (bootcamps, coaching, creative services, memberships) had "over 90" subscription members and raised ~$988K via Reg CF by April 2024 — a data point on the scale of a speaker-community business. — [Netcapital: Impact Eleven](https://netcapital.com/companies/impacteleven)
- A coaching-sales vendor's analysis of 828K DM conversations found a single follow-up to an engaged lead more than doubled booked calls (+106%). — [Setsmart coaching packages](https://setsmart.io/blog/coaching-packages)
- ChartMogul 2026 funnel math: credit-card-required trials yield ~10.5 paying customers per 1,000 visitors vs ~5 for freemium and ~3.6 for no-card trials. — [ChartMogul Conversion Report 2026](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)

### Inferences
- The TEDx product should copy the proven speaker-education funnel: free live "How to get selected for TEDx" workshop → free AI event-match/pitch score (PQL) → reverse trial → program purchase, with human outreach to high-intent free users (which OpenView/Lenny data associate with faster growth).
- An affiliate program at 30% recurring (capped at 12 months, like Podia) is market-standard for coaches/consultants who refer peers; 40% (GoHighLevel) is the aggressive end used for agency-style resellers.
- With a 47.7% show rate, planning math for a webinar funnel is roughly: 1,000 registrants → ~477 attendees → (no sourced purchase rate; see Gaps).
